# Hakbang: Step-by-Step Math Tutor for Dyscalculia and Intellectual Disability

## Core Difficulties

Dyscalculia affects approximately 3-6% of primary-age children with core deficits in both symbolic number processing (digits, numerals) and nonsymbolic magnitude representation (dot arrays, finger counting). Intellectual disability compounds these with working memory and generalization barriers.

**Key research:** Decarli et al. (2023) found severe dyscalculia characterized by core deficits in symbolic and nonsymbolic number sense. Psychological Science, 34(1):8-21. DOI:10.1177/09567976221097947 (ScienceDirect).

## Evidence-Based Intervention Practices

### Explicit Systematic Instruction
Meta-analysis of 42 studies found explicit instruction among the highest-effect instructional components for students with learning disabilities. Effective explicit instruction requires: (a) teacher demonstrates step-by-step strategy, (b) strategy specific to problem set, (c) students replicate the procedure.

Gersten & Chard (2009). Mathematics Instruction for Students With Learning Disabilities: A Meta-Analysis of Instructional Components. Review of Educational Research, 79(3):1202-1242. DOI:10.3102/0034654309334431 (Google Scholar).

### Concrete-Representational-Abstract Sequencing
Meta-analysis of 30 single-case studies showed CRA sequences with Tau-BC effect size 0.9965 (95% CI [0.9947, 0.9983], p < 0.0001), particularly effective for computation. Virtual manipulatives (touchscreen-based) show similar benefits.

### Number Line Training
Meta-analysis of 263 effect sizes across 10,576 children (ages 4-14) found correlation r = .443 between number line estimation and broader mathematical competence, increasing with age and stronger for fractions than whole numbers.

Schneider et al. (2018). Associations of Number Line Estimation With Mathematical Competence: A Meta-analysis. Child Development, 89(5):1467-1484. DOI:10.1111/cdev.13068 (Google Scholar).

### Schema-Based Instruction
Problem structure representation (teaching students to recognize and diagram problem types: combine, compare, change) showed effect size 2.637 for students with learning difficulties solving word problems. Naming problem structures bridges concrete and abstract understanding.

### Worked Examples with Fading
Backward fading (problem with steps shown, then completion problems, then full problems) effective for verbal problem-solving. Learning-disabled students show marked improvement in problem-solving abilities when worked examples precede independent practice.

### Constant Time Delay vs System of Least Prompts
Both strategies produce criterion-level responding in teaching academic skills to students with developmental delays. Constant time delay more efficient across sessions, trials, errors, and instructional time to criterion.

Hudson, Rivera & Grady (2018). Research on Mathematics Instruction with Students with Significant Cognitive Disabilities: Has Anything Changed? Research and Practice for Persons with Severe Disabilities, 43(2):85-103. DOI:10.1177/1540796918756601 (Google Scholar).

### Functional Mathematics for Intellectual Disability
Task analysis, systematic instruction, and concrete-representational progressions identified as core effective practices for moderate/severe intellectual disability. Functional skills (money, time, measurement) matter more than abstract numeracy.

Browder et al. (2008). A Meta-Analysis on Teaching Mathematics to Students with Significant Cognitive Disabilities. Exceptional Children, 74(4):407-432. DOI:10.1177/001440290807400401 (Google Scholar).

## Digital Interventions

### Meta-Analysis of Digital Interventions
Benavides-Varela et al. (2020) analyzed 15 randomized controlled studies (1,073 participants). Digital-based interventions improved mathematical performance with mean effect size 0.55. No evidence that videogames offer advantages over drilling and tutoring approaches.

Benavides-Varela et al. (2020). Effectiveness of digital-based interventions for children with mathematical learning difficulties: A meta-analysis. Computers & Education, 157:103953. DOI:10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103953 (ScienceDirect).

### Calcularis
Computer-based training automatizing number representations and supporting mental number line formation. Randomized trial (N=67, Grades 2-5): children assigned to Calcularis (42+ sessions, 20 min each over 13 weeks) showed higher benefits in arithmetic operations and number line estimation vs waiting control. Improvements stable 3 months post-training.

Kaser, Baschera & Kohn (2013). Design and evaluation of the computer-based training program Calcularis for enhancing numerical cognition. Frontiers in Psychology, 4:489. DOI:10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00489 (Google Scholar).

Kohn, Rauscher & Kucian (2020). Efficacy of a Computer-Based Learning Program in Children With Developmental Dyscalculia. What Influences Individual Responsiveness? Frontiers in Psychology, 11:1115. DOI:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01115 (Google Scholar).

### The Number Race
Adaptive game training on numerical comparison tasks at performance-level difficulty. Five weeks of training (30 min/day, 4 days/week) showed: subitizing speed increased by several hundred milliseconds, numerical comparison speed increased, subtraction accuracy increased 23%. Open-source and freely available.

Wilson et al. (2006). Principles underlying the design of "The Number Race": An adaptive computer game for remediation of dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions, 2:19. DOI:10.1186/1744-9081-2-19 (Google Scholar).

Wilson et al. (2006). An open trial assessment of "The Number Race": An adaptive computer game for remediation of dyscalculia. Behavioral and Brain Functions, 2:20. DOI:10.1186/1744-9081-2-20 (Google Scholar).

### IEEE Serious Game for Dyscalculia
Kalcal & Sert (2019). Design of Serious Game for Dyscalculia. 2019 International Conference on Intelligent Systems and Game Theory (ISGS), Istanbul. DOI:10.1109/isgs49501.2019.9047035 (IEEE Xplore).

## Step-by-Step Tutoring with Check Questions

Hakbang presents one problem-solving step per reply, followed by one check question. This aligns with constant time delay methodology (Hudson et al. 2018) and explicit instruction principles (Gersten & Chard 2009). Scaffolding research confirms spacing and time at each step improves accessibility for students with memory, attention, and comprehension challenges.

## Hakbang Design Rules

| Rule | Evidence Support | Source |
|------|---|---|
| Show concrete visuals (bibingka, coins, number line) before symbols | Strong | CRA meta-analysis (Tau 0.997); Decarli dyscalculia; Schneider number line r=.443 |
| One step per reply, then check question | Strong | Explicit instruction (Gersten); Constant time delay (Hudson) |
| Use simple language; name problem structures | Strong | Schema-based instruction effect 2.637; Browder intellectual disability |
| Bring money, time, measurement early | Strong | Functional math (Browder); authentic contexts |
| Teacher/AI models step; child does next | Moderate | Worked examples literature; Gersten explicit instruction |
| Child manipulates objects or touchscreen | Moderate | CRA concrete emphasis; virtual manipulatives literature |
| Track per-step accuracy; adjust difficulty up | Strong | Adaptive learning (Calcularis, Number Race mechanisms) |
| Fade concrete materials slowly | Strong | CRA fade studies; worked examples with fading |
| Repeat same problem type multiple times | Strong | Massed practice to fluency (Gersten); explicit instruction |
| Use Filipino language, pesos, jeepney contexts | Moderate | Schema-based authentic relevance; functional math real materials |

## Competency Prioritization

| Competency Code | Topic | Priority | Adaptation/Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1NA-Ib-1 | Count up to 20, identify 1 more/less | Tackle First | Number sense foundation (Schneider; Decarli) |
| 1NA-Ic-1 | Read and write numerals 1-10 | Tackle First | Link symbolic-nonsymbolic (Decarli dyscalculia core deficit) |
| 1NA-Id-1 | Represent numbers: blocks, number line | Tackle First | CRA foundation; Schneider number line evidence |
| 1NA-IIb-2 | Place value in 2-digit numbers | Tackle First | Critical bottleneck; prerequisite for regrouping |
| 1NA-Ih-1 | Addition sums to 20 using concrete | Tackle with Adaptation | Heavy concrete; slow fade (CRA Tau 0.997) |
| 1NA-IIf-1 | Add 2-digit + 1-digit no regrouping | Tackle with Adaptation | Base-ten blocks; representational visuals essential |
| 1NA-IIi-1 | Subtraction to 20 concrete/pictorial | Tackle with Adaptation | Multiple models: take-away and number line |
| 1NA-IIIe-1 | Unit fractions 1/2, 1/4 as parts | Tackle with Adaptation | Extended concrete: paper folding, shading; avoid symbols initially |
| 1NA-IIIf-1 | Money: recognize coins, determine value | Tackle First | Functional (Browder); real objects or high-fidelity images |
| 1MG-IIIi-1 | Read analog clock: hour, half-hour | Tackle First | Functional; concrete representation of abstract time |
| 2NA-IIi-1 | Repeated addition, equal groups | Tackle with Caution | Arrays and skip-counting before symbolic multiplication; inference |
| 2NA-IIc-2 | Subtraction with regrouping up to 100 | Tackle with Adaptation | Base-ten blocks mandatory; reverse "trade" shown visually; extended scaffolding |
| 4NA-IIg-1 | Dissimilar fractions; compare | Avoid or Defer | High cognitive load; defer until multiplication and division fluent; inference |
| 5NA-Ic-2 | Multiply fractions by fractions | Avoid or Defer | Requires solid fraction magnitude sense (rare in dyscalculia); inference |
| 6NA-Ii-1 | Ratio, proportion, percentages | Avoid or Defer | Abstract chain; defer to Grade 5+ with solid foundations; inference |

## Learning Sequence

1. 1NA-Ib-1 (count to 20)
2. 1NA-Ic-1 (read/write numerals)
3. 1NA-Id-1 (represent on number line and with blocks)
4. 1NA-IIb-2 (place value tens/ones)
5. 1NA-If-1, 1NA-If-2 (compare, order numbers)
6. 1NA-Ih-1 (addition to 20, concrete)
7. 1NA-IIf-1 (addition 2-digit + 1-digit, no regrouping)
8. 1NA-IIi-1 (subtraction to 20)
9. 1NA-IIIe-1 (unit fractions 1/2, 1/4)
10. 1NA-IIIf-1 (money: value)
11. 1MG-IIIi-1 (tell time: analog)
12. 2NA-IIi-1 (repeated addition, equal groups)
13. 2NA-IIj-1 (multiplication as repeated addition, arrays)
14. 2NA-IIIa-1 (multiply using 2,3,4,5,10 facts)
15. 2NA-IIc-2 (subtraction with regrouping)
