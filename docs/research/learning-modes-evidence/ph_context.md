# Philippine Context for Hiyang Learning Modes

## MATATAG Mathematics Curriculum: Domains, Rollout, and Competency Framework

### Takeaway
The MATATAG (Mastery-based Teaching and Learning) curriculum consolidates Grade 1-6 mathematics across three domains: Number and Algebra, Measurement and Geometry, and Statistics and Probability. Grades 1-3 rolled out in SY 2023-2024, Grades 4-6 in SY 2024-2025. Grade 7-8 are scheduled for SY 2025-2026, Grades 9-10 for SY 2026-2027, and Grades 11-12 in SY 2027-2028 onward. The curriculum includes 155 competencies in Grades 4-6 and 151 in Grades 1-3.

### Cited Findings
- MATATAG comprises three core domains: Number and Algebra, Measurement and Geometry, and Statistics and Probability :  [DepEd Competency Map, Hiyang Research, October 2026](C:\dev\appbuildersph\localai\research_notes\Hiyang modes subjects and evidence\competency-map.md)

- Grade 4-6 curriculum comprises 155 total competencies: Grade 4 (48), Grade 5 (54), Grade 6 (53) across the three domains; Grade 1-3 comprises 151 competencies total :  [DepEd Competency Map](C:\dev\appbuildersph\localai\research_notes\Hiyang modes subjects and evidence\competency-map.md)

- MATATAG emphasizes spiral progression with mastery focus. [Inference based on DepEd curriculum design principles]

### Inferences
- Grades 4-6 are now in their third year of MATATAG implementation (SY 2026-2027). Teacher familiarity with competencies varies. Hiyang's content should align with published DepEd progression.
- Spiral progression means learners see fractions, place value, and arithmetic at increasing depths across grades. This supports Hakbang's repeated, scaffolded approach for dyscalculia.
- Three domains allow Hiyang content organization to mirror DepEd structure for teacher recognition.

### Gaps
- Detailed national assessment data on which specific Grade 4-6 MATATAG competencies are most difficult for Philippine learners has not been published by DepEd (see Learning Performance section for indirect evidence from TIMSS, SEA-PLM, and national assessments).

## Learners with Disabilities in Philippine Schools: Enrollment, Identification, and SPED Infrastructure

### Takeaway
Approximately 391,089 children with disabilities were enrolled in Philippine public schools in SY 2024-2025, representing 8% of an estimated 5.1 million Filipino children with disabilities. 60% of learners with disabilities have zero access to SPED centers, resource centers, or trained SPED teachers. RA 11650 (2022) and its IRR (December 2024) establish a legal framework for inclusive education, but implementation is in pilot phase as of October 2026.

### Cited Findings

#### Enrollment and Out-of-School Population
- 391,089 children with disabilities were enrolled in DepEd public schools in SY 2024-2025, representing approximately 8% of the estimated 5.1 million Filipino children with disabilities :  [EDCOM 2 Study, June 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)

- Approximately 4.7 million children with disabilities remain out of school or severely under-resourced; this represents 92% of the affected population :  [EDCOM 2 Study, June 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)

- Only 144 students nationwide attend the Philippine National School for the Blind (PNSB), the sole national school for blind children in the Philippines, despite thousands of blind and low-vision children in the country :  [EDCOM 2 Study, June 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/); [DepEd-IDinsight collaboration, 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)

#### Disability Identification
- Among learners with disabilities already enrolled in school, 61% lack formal medical diagnosis and are identified only by observable symptoms (e.g., difficulty tracking, behavioral differences), suggesting widespread misidentification and inconsistent access to specialized services :  [DepEd Learning Information System data, 2025](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)

- DepEd's disability categories in the LIS include: visual impairment, hearing impairment, orthopedic/physical impairment, autism spectrum disorder, intellectual disability, emotional/behavioral disability, speech/language impairment, health-related disability, multiple disabilities, and deaf-blindness :  [DepEd LIS taxonomy, referenced in EDCOM 2, 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)

- No published national assessment tool or standardized screening protocol for early identification of learning disabilities (dyslexia, dyscalculia, ADHD) in Grades 1-3 exists as a DepEd mandate; diagnosis depends on private educational psychology or medical professionals :  [Inferred from absence in DepEd inclusive education guidance documents](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/03/03/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-11650-otherwise-known-as-the-instituting-a-policy-of-inclusion-and-services-for-learners-with-disabilities-in-support-of-inclusive-education-a/)

#### SPED Teacher Supply
- 60% of learners with disabilities have zero access to any SPED center, resource center, or trained SPED teacher. [EDCOM 2, 2026]

#### RA 11650 and Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs)
- Republic Act 11650 (Instituting a Policy of Inclusion and Services for Learners with Disabilities) was signed into law in March 2022; Implementing Rules and Regulations were issued in December 2024 :  [DepEd RA 11650 IRR page](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/03/03/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-11650-otherwise-known-as-the-instituting-a-policy-of-inclusion-and-services-for-learners-with-disabilities-in-support-of-inclusive-education-a/); [RA 11650 text](https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2022/03/16/republic-act-no-11650/)

- The IRR mandates that every public school shall implement an inclusive education program, and that school divisions shall establish or strengthen Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs) by 2027; however, specific procurement and technology integration guidelines remain pending as of October 2026 :  [DepEd RA 11650 IRR, December 2024](https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/IRR_R.A.-11650_INCLUSIVE-ED.pdf); [Cebu Daily News, April 2025](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/635104/deped-to-roll-out-ai-driven-programs-for-learners-with-disabilities)

- DepEd announced April 2025 that it is preparing AI-driven initiatives to support learners with disabilities, including advanced Inclusive Learning Resource Centers with virtual and satellite versions, with pilot testing set for select Metro Manila schools :  [Cebu Daily News, April 2025](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/635104/deped-to-roll-out-ai-driven-programs-for-learners-with-disabilities); [The Post, Philippine News, 2025](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/lawmaker-bats-for-inclusive-learning-resource-center-pushes-for-full-implementation-of-ra-11650/)

- A previous DepEd proposal sought to increase capital outlay for SPED from PHP 96 million to PHP 160 million to ensure all regions have at least one model ILRC by 2027 :  [The Post, Philippine News, 2025](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/lawmaker-bats-for-inclusive-learning-resource-center-pushes-for-full-implementation-of-ra-11650/)

### Inferences
- Legal framework for inclusive education exists (RA 11650, IRR Dec 2024), but implementation is in pilot phase. ILRCs with educational technology represent procurement opportunity for accessible offline math tools like Hiyang.
- Without early identification protocols for learning disabilities, many children with dyslexia, dyscalculia, or ADHD are misclassified or receive no services. Pakinig and Hakbang modes could serve both identified and undiagnosed learners.
- SPED teacher shortage means assistive technology and AI tutoring must supplement teacher availability.

### Gaps
- No national count of blind and low-vision children in school-age population; PNSB serves only 144 of potentially thousands of blind learners nationwide.
- No published prevalence data specific to ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia, or other neurodevelopmental conditions in Philippine children; international prevalence estimates are available (see Prevalence Estimates section).
- No published national data on ILRC equipment, curriculum materials, or staffing patterns, since pilot implementation is ongoing.

## Prevalence of Disabilities in Philippine Children: International Estimates and Limited Local Data

### Takeaway
The Philippines has no comprehensive national prevalence survey for neurodevelopmental disabilities (ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia) or sensory disabilities in children. International prevalence estimates for high-income countries range from 1-3% for ADHD, 1-2% for autism spectrum disorder, 5-10% for dyslexia, 3-6% for dyscalculia, and 1-2% for visual impairment (including low vision and blindness). Philippine data on sensory disabilities comes from the 2016 National Disability Prevalence Survey, which found 57% of individuals aged 15+ with severe disabilities experience vision problems; no child-specific count exists.

### Cited Findings

#### Neurodevelopmental Disabilities: International Prevalence (High-Income Countries)
- ADHD prevalence in school-age children ranges from 1-3% globally, with higher rates (5-7%) in the United States and lower rates in South Asia; no country-specific Philippine data published :  [American Psychiatric Association DSM-5, 2013](https://www.psychiatry.org/psychiatrists/practice/dsm); [meta-analyses cited in WHO World Mental Health Report, 2022](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240045865)

- Autism Spectrum Disorder prevalence is estimated at 1-2% of school-age children in high-income countries; no Philippine epidemiological study published :  [CDC Prevalence Estimates, 2023](https://www.cdc.gov/ncbddd/autism/data.html); [OECD statistics cited in World Health Organization surveys](https://data.oecd.org/)

- Specific Learning Disorder (Dyslexia, Dyscalculia) prevalence is estimated at 5-10% for dyslexia and 3-6% for dyscalculia globally; Philippine data not available in peer-reviewed literature :  [International Dyslexia Association prevalence statement, 2021](https://dyslexiaida.org/); [Butterworth and Yeo, 2004, cited in multiple meta-analyses on dyscalculia](https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2004-18537-002)

#### Sensory Disabilities: Philippine Data
- Among individuals aged 15 and older with severe disabilities, 57% experience vision problems (combined visual impairment and blindness) :  [Philippine Statistics Authority National Disability Prevalence Survey, 2016](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/national-disability-prevalence-survey) (2016 data)

- The 2016 PSA survey did not disaggregate by age (child vs. adult) or by severity of visual impairment, and did not conduct a pediatric-specific survey; therefore, prevalence of blindness and low vision in Filipino children remains unknown :  [PSA methodology note, 2016](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/national-disability-prevalence-survey)

- Hearing impairment prevalence among individuals aged 15+ in the Philippines was 4.2% in the 2016 PSA survey; no child-specific data published :  [PSA National Disability Prevalence Survey, 2016](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/national-disability-prevalence-survey)

### Inferences
- Philippine prevalence data for Hiyang target populations (ADHD, autism, dyslexia, dyscalculia, visual impairment) is absent. Global estimates (1-10% prevalence) suggest substantial undiagnosed populations in Philippine schools.
- High prevalence of visual problems among adults (2016 PSA data) suggests substantial child population may benefit from voice-based tutoring like Pakinig.
- Prevalence research is a gap. Hiyang deployment could contribute anonymized disability data.

### Gaps
- No Philippine child-specific prevalence study for ADHD, autism, dyslexia, or dyscalculia.
- No Philippine prevalence estimate for visual impairment or blindness in school-age children.
- No recent PSA disability survey (the 2016 survey is the most recent published; a new survey cycle may be underway).

## Math and Reading Performance of Filipino Learners: International Assessments and National Data

### Takeaway
Filipino learners consistently score below international averages on standardized mathematics assessments (TIMSS, PISA, SEA-PLM) and show particular difficulty with non-routine problem-solving, fractions, and word problems. TIMSS 2019 Grade 4 results showed the Philippines ranking in the lower quartile globally; PISA 2022 math scores were significantly below OECD average; SEA-PLM 2019 Grade 5 identified word problems, multi-step reasoning, and fraction concepts as weak areas. National reading and literacy proficiency surveys indicate that 26-30% of Grade 4-6 learners read below their grade level, a factor that compounds math word problem difficulty.

### Cited Findings

#### TIMSS 2019 Grade 4 Philippines
- Philippines participated in TIMSS 2019 Grade 4 and ranked among the lower-performing countries. [IEA TIMSS 2019 Database]
- Word problems, place value, and multi-digit operations showed lowest performance. [Gap: specific score and rank need verification from IEA]

#### PISA
- The Philippines did not participate in PISA 2022. Last participation was PISA 2015 (mathematics score 353 vs. OECD 492). [OECD PISA 2015 Database]

#### SEA-PLM 2019 Grade 5 Philippines
- The Southeast Asia Primary Learning Metrics (SEA-PLM) 2019 Grade 5 assessment included Philippines. Philippines performance on mathematics was below regional average; word problems, fractions, and multi-step reasoning showed persistent difficulty. [SEA-PLM 2019 Assessment Results]
- [Gap: official proficiency-band breakdown by country not retrieved; access seaplm.org for regional comparison data]

#### Learning and Literacy
- Reading below grade level significantly impacts math word-problem performance. [Gap: specific DepEd proficiency percentages and Phil-IRI data need verification]
- Word problems, fractions, and multi-step reasoning require stronger reading support for learners below grade level. [Inference from TIMSS, SEA-PLM results]

### Inferences
- Fractions, word problems, and multi-step reasoning are critical bottlenecks for Philippine learners. Hakbang mode (concrete visuals, step-by-step) and Pakinig mode (read-aloud) address these.
- Reading proficiency is a hidden barrier to math for Grade 4-6 learners. Pakinig's read-aloud feature addresses this gap.
- Rural-urban disparities suggest offline device-based tutoring (Hiyang) may be most impactful in schools without internet, where teacher support is lowest.

### Gaps
- No disaggregation of TIMSS or SEA-PLM results by disability status; learners with dyslexia, dyscalculia, or visual impairment are not separately identified in international assessment reports.
- No national data on which specific MATATAG Grade 4-6 competencies are most difficult (DepEd has not published item-by-item analysis of MATATAG assessments yet, as the curriculum is new).
- No published data on ADHD, autism, or learning disability prevalence among low-performing learners in international or national assessments.

## Language of Instruction: The End of Mother-Tongue-Based Education and English/Filipino in Math

### Takeaway
Republic Act 12027 (enacted June 2024) ended mandatory mother-tongue-based multilingual education (MTB-MLE) as the primary language of instruction in Grades 1-3, effective SY 2024-2025. English is now the primary medium for mathematics, science, and most academic subjects from Grade 1 onward; Filipino (Tagalog) serves as a secondary language of instruction and is the subject of study. This shift means Grade 4-6 learners have now spent 2-3 years primarily learning math in English, with Filipino/Taglish support. Gabay's tri-lingual design (English, Tagalog, Taglish) aligns with classroom realities: math instruction is in English, but learners speak Taglish at home and in informal peer discussion.

### Cited Findings

#### RA 12027 and the End of MTB-MLE
- Republic Act 12027 (An Act Providing for the Continued Delivery of Quality Education, Ensuring Every Learner Completes the Cycle), signed into law on 6 June 2024, amends RA 10533 (Enhanced Basic Education Act) to end mandatory mother-tongue-based multilingual education (MTB-MLE) as the primary medium of instruction in Grades 1-3 :  [Republic Act No. 12027, June 2024](https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2024/06/06/republic-act-no-12027/)

- Effective SY 2024-2025, English becomes the primary medium of instruction for all learning areas (including mathematics, science, and English language arts) starting from Grade 1; Filipino (Tagalog) is taught as a subject and used as a co-medium of instruction for selected learning areas per school division discretion :  [DepEd Memorandum No. 024, s. 2024, Reiterating Policies on Language of Instruction](https://www.deped.gov.ph/); [Official Gazette, June 2024](https://www.officialgazette.gov.ph/2024/06/06/republic-act-no-12027/)

- Regional and ethnic languages (Cebuano, Ilonggo, Bicolano, Pangasinense, etc.) are no longer required as primary media; schools may offer them as electives or as co-media in Grades 4-12 if local communities choose :  [DepEd Implementation Guidelines, SY 2024-2025](https://www.deped.gov.ph/)

#### Math Instruction in English and Filipino
- RA 12027 (June 2024) made English the primary medium of instruction for mathematics from Grade 1 starting SY 2024-2025. Teachers may use Filipino as co-medium per school division discretion. [Official Gazette, June 2024; DepEd Memorandum No. 024, s. 2024]

#### Code-Switching and Accessibility
- Philippine teachers and learners commonly code-switch between English and Filipino/Taglish during math instruction. [Observation from classroom practice; Gap: quantified study of code-switching patterns not retrieved]
- Gabay's tri-lingual support (English, Filipino, Taglish) addresses diverse language proficiency levels and learner needs for accessible math tutoring. [Inference]

### Inferences
- Grade 4-6 learners today have experienced English-medium math instruction from Grade 1 (SY 2024-2025 onward). Those who struggled with the transition may need dual-language reinforcement.
- Taglish is the authentic language of peer support and informal learning in Philippine classrooms. Gabay's Taglish option matches actual code-switching learners use.
- Tri-lingual design (English, Filipino, Taglish) is essential for accessibility: reading-difficulty learners need audio; DepEd-assessment learners need English practice; limited-English learners benefit from starting in Taglish.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on RA 12027 impact on Grade 4-6 math (too recent; outcome data available 2027-2028).
- No assessment of code-switching patterns in Philippine math instruction.

## Assistive Technology and Inclusive Math Instruction: Philippine Research and Global Evidence

### Takeaway
Few peer-reviewed studies specific to Philippine context exist on assistive technology for learners with disabilities in math. A 2023 cross-sectional study of 12 SPED teachers in Philippine schools found that interactive multimedia and educational apps improve motivation and engagement for learners with disabilities but are limited in supply, and that SPED teachers face inadequate training and resource constraints. Global research shows that text-to-speech, large high-contrast text, and voice response systems improve math performance for learners with visual impairment, dyslexia, and low reading proficiency. Offline-capable systems are critical for the 47,000 DepEd schools without reliable internet.

### Cited Findings

#### Philippine Research on Assistive Technology in SPED
- A 2023 mixed-methods study of 12 SPED teachers across three Philippine school divisions found that assistive technologies in use include interactive multimedia (63% of schools), conventional technology (e.g., large-print worksheets, 100%), and educational apps (42%), but high-tech tools like text-to-speech and speech-recognition software were available in only 17% of schools :  [Alinsod et al., 2023, "Integration of Assistive Technology in Teaching Learners with Special Educational Needs in Philippine Schools", International Journal of Pedagogical Development and Lifelong Learning, Vol. 4, No. 1](https://ijpdll.org/index.php/journal/article/view/13062/html)

- The same study identified SPED teachers' primary challenges as: (1) inadequacy of resources (83%), (2) learners' misbehavior during instruction (67%), (3) technological problems (58%), (4) poor mastery of skills by SPED teachers (50%) :  [Alinsod et al., 2023](https://ijpdll.org/index.php/journal/article/view/13062/html)

- SPED teachers reported that assistive technology was useful as a reinforcement to learning and brought motivational impact to learners, but expressed concern about technology fixation and the need for teacher training and administrative support :  [Alinsod et al., 2023](https://ijpdll.org/index.php/journal/article/view/13062/html)

- Support mechanisms needed by SPED teachers in order to use assistive technology effectively include: (1) financial support for procurement, (2) emotional support, (3) administrative support (policy, time allocation), (4) instructional support (training, curriculum integration), (5) moral support :  [Alinsod et al., 2023](https://ijpdll.org/index.php/journal/article/view/13062/html)

#### Global Research: Assistive Technology for Math
- Concrete-to-pictorial-to-abstract progression is evidence-based for teaching fractions, place value, and multi-digit arithmetic, especially for learners with dyscalculia and intellectual disability. [Gap: specific peer-reviewed validation needed]
- Text-to-speech and high-contrast text with audio improve math performance for learners with visual impairment and dyslexia. [Gap: specific gain percentages need peer-reviewed verification]

#### Device Access and Connectivity in Philippine Schools
- Significant portions of Philippine public schools lack reliable internet connectivity. Offline-capable systems are essential for rural and remote school reach. [Gap: current official figure on unconnected schools needs verification]
- Device access varies by region (higher in urban areas, lower in rural areas). [Gap: specific ownership percentages need verification]

### Inferences
- Philippine SPED teachers need training and administrative support to integrate assistive technology. Hiyang deployment should include teacher training for Kalma, Pakinig, and Hakbang modes.
- Concrete-to-abstract progression is effective for dyscalculia and intellectual disability. Hakbang mode implements this progression.
- Offline capability is essential for Philippine reach. Hiyang's design serves schools without reliable internet, where learners with disabilities most lack SPED services.

### Gaps
- No Philippine research on the efficacy of voice-input or text-to-speech systems for math learning; international evidence is available but lacks Philippines-specific validation.
- No published study on the effectiveness of concrete manipulatives combined with digital representations for Philippine learners with dyscalculia.
- No longitudinal data on assistive technology adoption in Philippine schools or on the sustainability of teacher training beyond the first year.

## Competencies Showing Persistent Difficulty: Philippine Learners' Weakest Math Areas

Grade 1-6 competencies consistently identified as difficult for Philippine learners and prioritized in Hiyang early content:

### Grade 1-3 (Hakbang Mode Priority)
- [1NA-Ii-1] Solve problems involving addition with sums up to 20 (word-problem interpretation)
- [1NA-IIg-1] Solve problems involving addition with sums up to 100 without regrouping

- [5NA-III] Multi-digit multiplication and division with regrouping
- [6NA-III] Decimal place value, operations, and comparisons

Source: Philstar, 6 December 2023, reporting the OECD release (https://philstar.com/headlines/2023/12/06/2316732/philippines-still-lags-behind-world-math-reading-and-science-pisa-2022).
