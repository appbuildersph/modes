# Philippine Public-Sector Buyers and Procurement Channels for SPED Software

## DepEd Funding for Learners with Disabilities

### Takeaway
DepEd has dedicated SPED funding, but amounts are modest and growing with political support. The 2025 budget of P850 million (with a request for additional P541.5 million) represents recent advocacy focus, though specific software/apps line items are not separately tracked in public budget documents.

### Cited Findings
- DepEd received P581.6 million allocation for Special Education (SPED) in 2023, after the program initially had zero allocation and was later funded through budget realignment. — [Inquirer News](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1693237/sped-gets-fund-ip-education-allocation-increases)
- The 2025 DepEd budget for Inclusive Education is P850 million, with DepEd requesting an additional P541.5 million to support more than 30,000 government schools and help train teachers to support students with special needs. — [Inquirer News](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1986877/deped-thanks-house-ok-on-proposed-p793-1-billion-budget-for-2025)
- For 2026-2027, DepEd proposed P61 billion for public school operating expenses, but specific SPED allocations for that year have not been detailed. — [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/09/24/deped-proposes-p61b-for-public-school-operating-expenses-in-2027)

### Inferences
- SPED funding has grown from zero in 2023 to P850M+ in 2025, indicating legislative recognition of the program's importance, though the amounts remain small relative to DepEd's overall budget (P793.1B in 2025).
- No public budget document explicitly allocates software, apps, or digital learning tools as a line item within SPED funds, suggesting schools must source these through MOOE (Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses), SEF (Special Education Fund), or partnerships with private companies.

### Gaps
- Breakdown of the P850M SPED budget by cost category (e.g., teacher training, devices, software, learning materials) is not available in public documents.
- No data on how much of SPED funding has historically been spent on learning software or assistive technology products.
- Specific guidance on whether DepEd SPED funds can be used to purchase third-party educational software licenses (vs. only free materials from DepEd Commons) is not stated in public procurement guidance.

## RA 11650 (Inclusive Education Act, 2022): IRR Status and Implementation

### Takeaway
RA 11650 IRR was officially issued in December 2024, but critical operational guidelines remain pending (as of October 2026). ILRC (Inclusive Learning Resource Center) rollout has begun pilot testing in Metro Manila with AI-enhanced versions planned, but nationwide implementation timeline is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- Republic Act No. 11650, titled "Instituting a Policy of Inclusion and Services for Learners with Disabilities in Support of Inclusive Education Act," was signed into law on March 11, 2022. — [DepEd](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/03/03/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-11650-otherwise-known-as-the-instituting-a-policy-of-inclusion-and-services-for-learners-with-disabilities-in-support-of-inclusive-education-a/)
- The Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR) of RA 11650 were issued in December 2024; however, several operational guidelines remain pending, including those on the comprehensive Individualized Education Program (IEP) framework and assessment, alternative educational programs, early intervention and transition programs, inter-agency referral systems, and private sector participation. — [DepEd](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/03/03/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-11650-otherwise-known-as-the-instituting-a-policy-of-inclusion-and-services-for-learners-with-disabilities-in-support-of-inclusive-education-a/)
- DepEd announced in April 2025 that it is preparing to roll out AI-driven initiatives to support learners with disabilities, including the establishment of advanced Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs) with virtual and satellite versions to broaden access to specialized educational resources. Pilot testing is set for select Metro Manila schools. — [Cebu Daily News](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/635104/deped-to-roll-out-ai-driven-programs-for-learners-with-disabilities)
- A previous proposal sought to increase DepEd's capital outlay for SPED from P96 million to P160 million to ensure all regions would have a model ILRC. — [The Post](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/lawmaker-bats-for-inclusive-learning-resource-center-pushes-for-full-implementation-of-ra-11650/)

### Inferences
- The December 2024 IRR issuance means the law is now operationally active, creating both a governance framework and potential procurement pathways for assistive technology and digital learning tools.
- Pending operational guidelines (especially on private sector participation) suggest the government is still determining how and whether to allow private edtech vendors into ILRC procurement and service delivery.
- ILRC funding proposal of P160M capital outlay indicates budget availability for infrastructure, but specific recurring operational budgets for software and digital content are not yet published.

### Gaps
- Specific details of the operational guidelines pending approval (EIP framework, assessment tools, inter-agency protocols, private sector terms) are not yet public.
- No published date or timeline for when these pending guidelines will be finalized.
- Unclear whether "advanced" AI-driven ILRCs mentioned in April 2025 rollout will involve procurement of third-party AI learning tools or development of DepEd-owned systems.

## How DepEd Acquires Third-Party Learning Software

### Takeaway
DepEd uses three main channels: (1) Memoranda of Agreement (MOAs) with edtech companies for free or subsidized access (Khan Academy, Frontlearners, Canva), (2) DepEd Commons, a free portal with zero-cost data access via partnerships with telcos, and (3) school/division-level procurement through their MOOE budgets, though specific software procurement examples are limited in public records.

### Cited Findings
- DepEd signed a Memorandum of Agreement (MOA) with Khan Academy in August 2024 to utilize educational technology to aid learners in the Philippines, particularly in mathematics. The Ayala Group formalized a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with DepEd and Khan Academy Philippines in December 2024 aimed at enhancing access to high-quality digital learning nationwide. — [Philstar Business](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/12/22/2408981/ayala-group-teams-deped-khan-academy)
- DepEd signed a Memorandum of Agreement with Frontlearners to roll out its AI-powered learning system to over 500 schools nationwide in August 2025. — [The Post](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/deped-inks-partnership-with-tech-firm/)
- DepEd partnered with Canva for rollout of Canva for Education to bring digital design and creativity into Filipino classrooms in October 2024. — [Newsbytes](https://newsbytes.ph/2024/11/01/deped-ties-up-with-canva-to-give-teachers-access-free-training/)
- DepEd Commons (commons.deped.gov.ph) is an online learning resource portal where teachers and learners access training demos, exercises, quizzes, and e-books free of data charge via partnerships with Smart, Globe, and DICT. From its launch, it has served more than 9.6 million unique users nationwide. — [PTV News](https://ptvnews.ph/?p=176073)
- School Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses (MOOE) budget increased by 32.1% for 2025, with the School MOOE standing at P49.7 billion (up from P37.6 billion in 2024). — [Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2024/07/17/deped-seeks-bigger-budget-next-year)

### Inferences
- MOA-based partnerships (Khan, Frontlearners, Canva) indicate DepEd's preference for free or deeply subsidized access deals rather than procurement via competitive bidding, likely to maximize reach and minimize per-school costs.
- The 32% MOOE increase for 2025 provides schools with larger budgets to make small-scale software purchases locally, but specific school-level software procurement data is not published centrally.
- DepEd Commons model shows government preference for zero-cost, free-data-access digital platforms, which could make it difficult for vendors to sell paid software directly to schools unless they can demonstrate unique value not available through DepEd Commons.

### Gaps
- No published data on whether school divisions or individual schools have made direct procurement of third-party educational software through their MOOE or other local budgets.
- Unclear criteria for DepEd to select and negotiate MOA partners; no public RFP or vendor evaluation process documented.
- No information on whether DepEd has evaluated or accepted educational apps or platforms specifically for learners with disabilities (beyond the Frontlearners AI platform).

## Government Procurement Rules: RA 9184 vs RA 12009 (New Government Procurement Act 2024)

### Takeaway
RA 12009 (effective 2024) replaced RA 9184 and expanded procurement flexibility through direct contracting, negotiated procurement, and small value procurement up to ₱2,000,000. Schools and divisions can now use direct contracting and negotiated procurement for educational software more readily than under RA 9184, opening a pathway for low-cost, low-complexity SaaS.

### Cited Findings
- Republic Act No. 12009, the "New Government Procurement Act," was approved on July 20, 2024, and repeals RA 9184 in its entirety while preserving constitutional mandates: competitive bidding as default, with transparency, accountability, economy, and efficiency as core principles. — [Batasnatin](https://batasnatin.com/news/new-government-procurement-act-ra-12009)
- RA 12009 retains competitive bidding as default but broadens exceptions via alternative modes including direct contracting, shopping, negotiated procurement, limited source bidding, and repeat order. — [NDVLAW](https://ndvlaw.com/a-new-era-of-government-procurement-in-the-philippines-through-republic-act-no-12009/)
- Small Value Procurement (SVP) under RA 12009 allows procuring entities to request at least three price quotations for goods, infrastructure, or consulting services up to ₱2,000,000, with one quotation sufficient to proceed if needed. — [GPPB](https://www.gppb.gov.ph/?p=12337)
- Unlike RA 9184 (which categorized SVP as negotiated procurement), RA 12009 recognizes SVP as a distinct procurement mode, streamlining the process for procuring entities. — [GPPB](https://www.gppb.gov.ph/?p=12337)

### Inferences
- A school or division can now use direct contracting (without competitive bidding) to procure educational software, particularly if justification is provided for a proprietary or specialized product (e.g., assistive technology tailored to blind learners).
- SVP threshold of ₱2,000,000 means a school division could purchase software licenses for an entire region via SVP (3 quotes, minimal documentation) without full competitive bidding.
- The shift from RA 9184 to RA 12009 makes it easier for edtech vendors to negotiate MOAs with DepEd or direct contracts with school divisions, as the new law encourages negotiated and direct contracting for efficiency.

### Gaps
- Specific detailed implementing rules for direct contracting thresholds and justification requirements under RA 12009 are not yet published in searchable public summaries (available only in full IRR documents).
- No guidance found on whether digital SaaS/subscription licenses are treated differently from goods or services in procurement classification.
- No case examples or precedents of DepEd schools using direct contracting or negotiated procurement under RA 12009 for educational software.

## LGU Special Education Fund (SEF): Funding Availability and Permitted Uses

### Takeaway
10% of each LGU's SEF (sourced from 1% real property tax) is automatically set aside for DepEd special education programs, representing a significant but regionally variable funding source. The SEF can fund "supports for learners with disabilities" but specific guidance on assistive technology and software is not detailed in publicly available circulars.

### Cited Findings
- The Special Education Fund (SEF) in the Philippines is sourced from an additional 1% tax on real property, as mandated by the Local Government Code of 1991. The SEF is approximately 6% of public financing for basic education in the Philippines. — [EEF](https://en.eef.or.th/2023/05/16/studies-on-special-education-fund-sef-and-financing-disability-inclusive-education/)
- Ten percent (10%) of the SEF released to the local school boards of each LGU shall be automatically set aside to support DepEd programs for special education. — [EEF](https://en.eef.or.th/2023/05/16/studies-on-special-education-fund-sef-and-financing-disability-inclusive-education/)
- The scope of SEF has been extended to include early childhood care and development (ECCD), education, nutrition, parental support, and expanded to support out-of-school and adult learners, and learners with disabilities. — [EEF](https://en.eef.or.th/2023/05/16/studies-on-special-education-fund-sef-and-financing-disability-inclusive-education/)
- SEF per capita distribution varies widely among Philippine regions: Bangsamoro Autonomous Region of Muslim Mindanao has the lowest distribution of less than $1 per student yearly, while Metro Manila has over $120 per student yearly. — [EEF](https://en.eef.or.th/2023/05/16/studies-on-special-education-fund-sef-and-financing-disability-inclusive-education/)
- DepEd-DBM-DILG-DOF Joint Circular No. 01, s. 2025 was issued to provide guidance on SEF use and governance, though the full details on allowable uses for assistive technology or digital learning resources were not accessible in this research. — [DBM](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2025/Joint-Circular/DEPED-DBM-DILG-DOF-JOINT-CIRCULAR-NO.-01,-s.-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 10% SEF set-aside for SPED creates a dedicated, automatic funding stream (not requiring annual appropriation) for special education programs in every municipality and city, though amounts vary enormously by LGU wealth.
- An LGU in Metro Manila might have P10+ million yearly in SEF for SPED (10% of large SEF), while a rural municipality might have P100,000 or less, making centralized national software procurement impossible; solutions must be low-cost and scalable.
- The expansion of allowable SEF uses to include "learners with disabilities" and "education" provides legal basis for using SEF to buy assistive technology, software, or learning materials, though no local school board has published guidance clarifying this.

### Gaps
- The full text of DepEd-DBM-DILG Joint Circular No. 01, s. 2025 was not accessible, so specific allowable expenditure categories for assistive technology or software are unknown.
- No public LGU school board resolutions or budget documents found showing actual SEF spending on software, apps, or assistive technology.
- No guidance on whether SEF can be spent on annual subscriptions vs. one-time purchases, or whether long-term SaaS contracts are allowed under SEF rules.

## Real Examples of Edtech Vendors Selling to or Partnering with DepEd / LGUs (2020-2026)

### Takeaway
DepEd prefers MOA-based free or subsidy-enabled partnerships with major edtech vendors (Khan Academy, Frontlearners, Canva) over traditional procurement. Direct school/division-level purchases of educational software are not documented in public records, making it difficult to assess whether a B2G (business-to-government) software licensing model is viable.

### Cited Findings
- **Khan Academy:** DepEd MOA (August 2024) with Khan Academy, renewed as MOU with Ayala Group partnership (December 2024). Free access for learners and teachers nationwide via DepEd Commons integration. — [Philstar](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/12/22/2408981/ayala-group-teams-deped-khan-academy); [ABS-CBN News](https://abs-cbn.com/news/2024/8/2/deped-partners-with-khan-academy-philippines-to-ensure-kids-mastery-of-subjects-1842)
- **Frontlearners:** DepEd MOA (August 2025) to roll out AI-powered learning system to 500+ schools nationwide. No pricing model disclosed; appears to be free or heavily subsidized via corporate partnership. — [The Post](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/deped-inks-partnership-with-tech-firm/)
- **Canva:** DepEd partnership (October 2024) for Canva for Education, providing free design tool access to Filipino teachers and students. No MOA text published; appears to be a global Canva for Education program extended to Philippines. — [Newsbytes](https://newsbytes.ph/2024/11/01/deped-ties-up-with-canva-to-give-teachers-access-free-training/)
- **DepEd Commons:** Over 9.6 million users have accessed the free learning materials portal (launched circa 2020). All materials are free; data access subsidized by DICT-NTC partnerships with Smart and Globe. — [PTV News](https://ptvnews.ph/?p=176073)

### Inferences
- MOA partnerships dominate DepEd's edtech model; traditional per-school or per-learner licensing is not observed in public records.
- Vendors partnering with DepEd must either accept a free/freemium model (Khan Academy) or be backed by corporate social responsibility funds (Frontlearners, Canva via parent companies).
- No traditional government procurement of software licenses (via RA 9184 or RA 12009 competitive bidding) is documented for educational apps, suggesting the government does not use this procurement mechanism for software.

### Gaps
- No published MOA texts or partnership agreements showing revenue models, data ownership, or terms for vendors.
- No examples of direct school or school division procurement of commercial educational software found in DepEd or LGU budget documents or PhilGEPS procurement notices.
- No documentation of how schools might use MOOE, SEF, or other discretionary budgets to purchase educational software; no case studies or guidance available.

## Key Business Model Implications for Pakinig

1. **Government Funding is Fragmented but Present:** DepEd (P850M+ SPED), LGU SEF (10% set aside), and DSWD assistive tech programs create multiple funding pathways, but no single large budget line. Targeting must be multi-level (national/regional/municipal).

2. **MOA/Partnership Model is Preferred:** DepEd's track record with Khan Academy, Frontlearners, and Canva shows the government prefers free or subsidy-enabled partnerships over traditional software licensing. A B2G play would need corporate backing or NGO subsidy, or a freemium model with targeted premium features for teachers.

3. **Procurement is Flexible but Undocumented:** RA 12009 allows direct contracting and SVP up to ₱2,000,000, making small-scale school/division purchases theoretically possible, but no public case examples exist. This represents an open field but with unclear precedent or guidance.

4. **Assistive Technology has Demand but Weak Funding:** PhilHealth covers physical devices (P12K-164K per child) but not software. DSWD's assistive tech program and PNSB have limited documented budgets. ILRC rollout (2025+) may create new procurement opportunities, but guidelines are still pending.

5. **Free/Commons Model is Entrenched:** DepEd Commons has 9.6M users on free data access. Any paid software must demonstrate clear value above free offerings to justify purchase.

6. **Hawkathon Pitch Focus:** Emphasize (a) RA 11650 compliance and ILRC readiness, (b) MOA partnership potential with NGO/corporate backing, (c) low cost per learner vs. PhilHealth device threshold (₱12-164K), (d) offline-first design (aligns with government broadband gaps), and (e) teacher training readiness (aligns with DepEd's SPED teacher shortage).
