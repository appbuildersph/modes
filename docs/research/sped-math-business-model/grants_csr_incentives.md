# Philippine Funding for Accessible EdTech: Grants, CSR, and Tax Incentives

## Adopt-a-School Program Act (RA 8525) and DepEd Partnerships

### Takeaway
RA 8525 offers 50% additional tax deduction on donations to public schools (capped at total deduction), but does not explicitly cover software as a qualifying contribution. Recent 2025 regulations (RR 013-2025) streamline procedures but retain the focus on physical infrastructure and training.

### Cited Findings
- Tax incentive: Private donors can deduct 100% of direct and exclusive contributions to public schools, plus 50% additional deduction; total deduction cannot exceed net income — [RA 8525](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8525_1998.html)
- BIR Revenue Regulation 013-2025 (March 31, 2025) consolidates procedures for tax exemptions under RA 8525, EBET Framework Act (RA 12063), and the Tax Code — [BIR Policy](https://serp-p.pids.gov.ph/publication/public/view?slug=bir-issues-policy-on-grant-of-tax-perks-under-ebet-adopt-school-programs)
- 100% additional deduction applies to donations to public schools under the streamlined rules — [DepEd announcement April 2025](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/04/25/boost-sa-pbbm-education-agenda-deped-suportado-ang-tax-incentive-reform-para-sa-adopt-a-school/)
- DepEd's Adopt-a-School portal exists for companies to partner directly with public schools — [DepEd Adopt-a-School](https://www.deped.gov.ph/about-adopt-a-school/)

### Inferences
- RA 8525 is designed for in-kind donations (materials, training, infrastructure) and cash gifts to public school institutions, not licensing fees for software. A tech product could be structured as a donation of devices or training services to qualify.
- The 50-100% additional deduction makes RA 8525 partnerships attractive for corporate tax planning, particularly for large companies with high net income that can absorb the full deduction.
- DepEd's Adopt-a-School office is the entry point; partnerships should be framed as school improvement initiatives (e.g., donation of tablets + pre-installed app + teacher training).

### Gaps
- No published guidance clarifying whether educational software (including open-source or non-commercial apps) qualifies as a deductible contribution under RA 8525.
- No data on typical donation amounts or number of companies currently using RA 8525 for edtech partnerships.
- Eligibility of international donors or foreign-registered entities not specified in search results.

## Magna Carta for Persons with Disabilities (RA 7277, RA 9442, RA 10524) and Tax Incentives

### Takeaway
RA 9442 (2007) and RA 10524 create VAT and income tax exemptions for PWDs and 20% purchase discounts on assistive devices. Local manufacturing of assistive technology is designated a preferred investment with incentives under the Omnibus Investments Code, but no direct funding or subsidies for private sector development of accessible edtech are mentioned in current law.

### Cited Findings
- PWDs receive minimum 20% discount on assistive devices (wheelchairs, crutches, hearing aids, prosthetics, etc.) and exemption from 12% VAT on the discounted amount — [RA 9442, RA 10754](https://worlddisabilityunion.com/images/international_law/FILEcfa232f783fdd9c.pdf)
- Local manufacturing of assistive technologies and technical aids is classified as a preferred area of investment under the Omnibus Investments Code (EO 226) and qualifies for investment incentives — [RA 9442](https://www.lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2007/ra_9442_2007.html)
- VAT exemptions and discounts apply to prescription drugs, medical/dental services, diagnostic/lab fees, and assistive devices — [RA 7277 as amended](https://lpr.adb.org/resource/magna-carta-disabled-persons-amended-republic-act-no-9442-philippines)

### Inferences
- RA 9442's "preferred investment" status for local assistive tech manufacturing suggests a company that produces or distributes accessibility-focused hardware (e.g., specialized tablets, audio devices) could apply for BOI incentives, not grants directly.
- The 20% VAT discount creates a revenue floor for assistive device sellers; Pakinig (as a free PWA) would not trigger this mechanism, but bundled with affordable hardware it might.
- Tax breaks flow to PWD consumers, not developers. There is no incentive pool for companies to build accessible edtech itself.

### Gaps
- No specific amounts or grant programs for private companies developing assistive technology software.
- No published Board of Investments (BOI) application requirements or approval rates for assistive tech startups.
- Unclear whether a digital-only product (PWA, app) qualifies as "local manufacturing" under the tax code.

## Government Startup and Innovation Funding (RA 11337, DOST, DICT, DTI)

### Takeaway
The Innovative Startup Act (RA 11337) funds startups at POC to MVP stages via DICT (P500k–P1M), DOST (up to P5M), and PCIEERD (unlimited, deadline July 31, 2026). Education technology is an explicitly named focus area for PCIEERD. Startup must be DTI/SEC registered for 1–7 years and have proof of concept.

### Cited Findings
- DICT Startup Grant Fund (Cohort 2, opened July 2024): P500,000 to P1,000,000 equity-free grants for startups in POC-to-prototype or prototype-to-MVP stages — [DICT SGF FAQ](https://startup.gov.ph/dict-startup-grant-fund-frequently-asked-questions/)
- DOST Startup Grant Fund 2025: up to P5 million per startup, managed by DOST-PCAARRD — [DOST-PCAARRD 2026 call](https://www.pcaarrd.dost.gov.ph/index.php/quick-information-dispatch-qid-articles/dost-pcaarrd-opens-call-for-2026-startup-grant-fund-program)
- PCIEERD Startup Grant Fund Program (deadline July 31, 2026) funds edtech, AI, IoT, and clean tech startups; focus areas include Education Technologies — [PCIEERD call](https://fundsforcompanies.fundsforngos.org/grant/call-for-proposals-pcieerd-startup-grant-fund-program-philippines/)
- ISA Steering Committee (DOST, DICT, DTI) has mobilized P2.1 billion across 212 startups as of 2025 — [DICT Startup Grant Fund](https://assistance.ph/dict-startup-grant-fund-sgf-program/)
- RA 11337 Innovative Startup Act; IRR published November 2019 — [RA 11337](https://www.digest.ph/laws/innovative-startup-act)

### Inferences
- Pakinig would likely qualify for DICT SGF Cohort 3 (dates TBD) or DOST SGF 2026 if: (1) registered as a startup with DTI or SEC, (2) has a working prototype (offline Whisper + answer checking), and (3) demonstrates market traction (e.g., pilot in a school or strong mentor endorsement).
- PCIEERD's "Education Technologies" focus makes it the most favorable pathway; P5M+ is possible if product roadmap includes scaling to multiple schools/regions.
- No equity required; founders retain 100% ownership, making these grants attractive for founder-led teams.

### Gaps
- No published list of DICT SGF Cohort 2 awardees or their sectors, so no edtech precedent visible.
- Eligibility of PWA (progressive web app) vs. native app not specified; likely acceptable if functional prototype exists.
- Application timelines and approval rates for DOST/PCIEERD not disclosed.
- Success rate for education technology startups unknown.

## Corporate Foundations in Education and Disability

### Takeaway
SM Foundation has the most explicit disability program (SM Cares PWD), with annual events and sensitivity training partnerships. Ayala Foundation, Jollibee Group, and Globe Foundation focus on education and scholarships. No specific disability-focused education grants (e.g., learning tech for PWD students) are published by these foundations; partnerships are typically in-kind or limited to scholarship support.

### Cited Findings
- SM Foundation: Program on Persons with Disabilities promotes barrier-free environments in SM malls; programs include sensitivity training, Angels Walk for Autism, Happy Walk for Down Syndrome, Emergency Preparedness Forums, Movies for the Blind and Deaf — [SM Cares PWD](https://www.smsupermalls.com/smcares/advocacies/persons-with-disabilities)
- SM Foundation partners with Autism Society Philippines, Down Syndrome Association, Deaf Blind Support Philippines, and other organizations — [SM Cares partnerships](https://www.smsupermalls.com/smcares/pwd)
- Ayala Foundation programs: Community Development, Leadership Development, Arts and Culture — [Ayala Foundation Programs](https://ayalafoundation.org/programs/)
- Jollibee Group Foundation ACE Scholarship Program: supports underprivileged youth with education and life skills training — [Jollibee Foundation](https://foundation.jollibeegroup.com/programs)
- SM Foundation, Ayala Foundation, and others supported by 2025 AmCham Corporate Social Impact Awards for education initiatives — [AmCham CSI Awards 2025](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/cbb/content/967392/amcham-philippines-recognizes-outstanding-csr-programs-at-the-2025-corporate-social-impact-awards/)

### Inferences
- SM Foundation's PWD focus is accessibility/inclusion (mall access, events) rather than assistive tech or learning tech. A pitch to SM might frame Pakinig as part of their disability inclusion mission.
- Ayala and Jollibee target economic empowerment and scholarships, not assistive tech; fit is indirect.
- None of the major foundations publish grant applications for edtech startups; engagement is likely through corporate partnership proposals or in-kind donation requests rather than open calls.

### Gaps
- No published amounts for SM Foundation disability programs or application processes.
- Ayala, Jollibee, Globe, Unilab, Metrobank, GT, Lopez, Coca-Cola, Aboitiz CSR budgets for education/disability not disclosed in search results.
- Hackathon sponsors (PC Express, AMD, ASUS, Cognition, Tutorials Dojo, PDAX, WhiteCloak) have no visible education or disability CSR programs in search results.

## International Grants and Programs Open to Philippine Teams (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
UNICEF Venture Fund and AT2030 accept Philippine teams and focus on innovation in accessibility and assistive technology, but current open calls are not sector-specific. USAID, ADB, and World Bank offer country-level education financing to Philippines but through government partnerships, not direct grants to startups. Google.org, Microsoft AI for Accessibility, Zero Project, and KOICA were not confirmed as having open calls for Philippine edtech teams in 2025–2026 via search results.

### Cited Findings
- UNICEF Venture Fund 2024 Annual Report confirms AI for Accessibility as a core impact area; focus on speech technology and language access — [UNICEF Venture Fund AR 2024](https://www.unicefventurefund.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/AR%202024%20-%20External.pdf)
- AT2030 (UK Aid-funded, led by Global Disability Innovation Hub) operates in 40+ countries including Philippines; AT Impact Fund provides flexible grants and venture-building support (Scale Studio) for assistive tech startups — [AT2030 AT Impact Fund](https://at2030.org/at-impact-fund/)
- AT2030 working on financing mechanism for assistive technologies through Philippine Health Insurance Corporation — [AT2030 Philippines work](https://at2030.org/static/at2030_core/outputs/Global-Developments-in-Assistive-Technology-B-21.pdf)
- Zero Project (Austrian-led) Call for Nominations 2027 (#ZeroCall27) in three areas: Independent Living, Political Participation, Assistive Technology; open to innovations globally — [Zero Project Awards 2027](https://zeroproject.org/)
- World Bank: P600 million ($600 million) for Project for Learning Upgrade Support and Decentralization (PLUS-D) to improve foundational literacy and numeracy for 21 million K-10 students (channel: government contracts, not startup grants) — [World Bank PLUS-D](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/04/03/world-bank-backs-better-learning-for-21-million-filipino-students)
- ADB: P256 billion (~$4.8B USD) in loans and grants to Philippines in 2025; education component not separately itemized — [ADB PH funding 2025](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1273558)
- USAID: P1.6 billion ($30M) five-year UPSKILL partnership for higher education (launched 2024); K–12 program not detailed — [USAID UPSKILL](https://www.philstar.com/headlines/2024/02/22/2335200/usaid-allots-p16-billion-philippines-higher-education)
- Google.org: Historical USD250,000 grant for Philippines edtech (2020); no 2025–2026 edtech call confirmed in search results — [Google.org PH historical grant](https://mb.com.ph/2020/08/24/google-delivers-four-initiatives-to-support-inclusive-distance-learning-in-the-philippines/)

### Inferences
- AT2030 is the most accessible entry point: explicitly funds assistive tech in low/middle-income countries and is active in Philippines.
- UNICEF Venture Fund likely accepts applications from Philippine social enterprises but requires equity-free model and open-source commitment (per standard UNICEF terms); not confirmed for 2025–2026 open call.
- World Bank and ADB funding flows through government (DepEd) contracts and budgets; a startup would need DepEd adoption first.
- Google.org and Microsoft AI for Accessibility (no 2025–2026 call found) are not confirmed accessible to startups in current cycle.
- Zero Project is a nomination/awards platform (April deadline), not a direct funding source; recognition could unlock other sponsorship.

### Gaps
- UNICEF Venture Fund 2025–2026 application deadlines and amounts not found.
- AT2030 AT Impact Fund application process, typical grant size, and approval timeline not detailed.
- Microsoft AI for Accessibility, Google.org, JICA, Korea KOICA, Australia DFAT current Philippines programs and startup eligibility not confirmed.
- USAID education funding post-2025 budget cuts not clarified; higher ed focus may exclude K–12 startups.

- Google.org's 2020 grant to educators (INCO/AIJC) shows openness to pedagogy-focused projects; a follow-up for Pakinig might frame the app as teacher professional development tool.

### Gaps
- No publicly named Philippine blind/low-vision education startup found in search results.
- No data on DICT SGF or DOST SGF awardees by sector; edtech precedents not visible.
- Prior funding sources and history of offline math prototypes in this space not confirmed.

## Summary and Recommended Next Steps

**Most accessible funding pathways (in order of immediacy):**

1. **DICT Startup Grant Fund Cohort 3** (dates TBD, watch [startup.gov.ph](https://startup.gov.ph/)): P500k–P1M, 4–6 month typical timeline, no equity loss. **Prerequisite:** DTI startup registration + working prototype.

2. **DOST Startup Grant Fund 2026** (call typically opens mid-year): Up to P5M, longer timeline. Requires DOST accreditation and research/innovation narrative. **Prerequisite:** DTI registration + proof of concept + team with prior grants or IP.

3. **PCIEERD Startup Grant Fund** (deadline July 31, 2026): Explicit education technology focus, no published cap. **Prerequisite:** Same as DOST.

4. **AT2030 AT Impact Fund** (ongoing, UK Aid-backed): No published cap; venture-building support included. **Check:** [at2030.org](https://at2030.org/) for current application window and contact local Philippines partner.

5. **RA 8525 Adopt-a-School** (immediate, partner-dependent): Partner with a public school and donate Pakinig + devices + teacher training; donor (corporate partner) gets 50–100% tax deduction. **Prerequisite:** School letter of interest + corporate sponsor identified.

**Tax incentive pathways (for corporate sponsors, not direct startup funding):**

- **RA 8525:** Structure as school donation (requires corporate partner), 50–100% deduction of contribution.
- **RA 9442 local assistive tech manufacturing:** Pursue BOI incentives if bundling app with hardware; not applicable for PWA alone.

**International (longer timeline, high bar):**

- **UNICEF Venture Fund:** Monitor [unicefventurefund.org](https://www.unicefventurefund.org/) for Philippines calls; open-source requirement likely. Typical range: USD50k–500k+ per venture.
- **AT2030:** Most likely fit; contact Global Disability Innovation Hub for Philippines program details.
- **Zero Project 2027 Awards:** No direct funding; recognition could unlock secondary sponsorship.

**Status as of October 9, 2026:** RA 11337 frameworks are actively funded; DICT Cohort 2 closed (Cohort 3 TBD), DOST 2026 call expected soon, PCIEERD deadline is July 31, 2026. No evidence of reduction or sunset of these programs.
