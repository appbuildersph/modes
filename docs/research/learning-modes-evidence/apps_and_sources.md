# Hiyang Learning Modes: Benchmark Apps and Research Evidence

## Key Question 1: Existing Apps and Tools Serving Hiyang's Three Learning Modes

### Takeaway

A fragmented ecosystem of apps serves learners with ADHD/autism (Kalma), dyslexia/low vision (Pakinig), and dyscalculia/intellectual disability (Hakbang). Peer-reviewed evidence exists for specialized dyscalculia tools (Calcularis, onebillion) and one ADHD game (EndeavorRx), but no peer-reviewed evidence base for "calm interface" ADHD apps. Hiyang combines offline-first design, on-device AI tutor, mode-specific pedagogies (Kalma calm, Pakinig audio, Hakbang concrete), and Filipino/Taglish support. No competitor combines all four.

### Cited Findings

#### Kalma Mode: ADHD/Autism Learning Apps

**EndeavorRx** (Akili Interactive; FDA-authorized digital therapeutic for children 8-12 with primarily inattentive or combined-type ADHD). Randomized controlled trial published in The Lancet Digital Health measured attention function via computer-based testing. Game mechanics: navigate aircraft through alien environments. Requires prescription in US. Online gameplay (not explicitly offline). Design: ages 8-12 children with ADHD; measured attention, not mathematics outcomes.

Peer-reviewed evidence: DOI 10.1016/s2589-7500(20)30017-0 (The Lancet Digital Health, 2020). [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/the-lancet-digital-health)

**Autism Learning Games For Kids** (iOS app ID 6747318529). Designed for children with autism; simple interface, no animations, no timers, no competitive elements. No peer-reviewed evidence found. Product documentation only.

**Generic ADHD-friendly math characteristics**: Short puzzles, calm visuals, hands-on play, reduced sensory overload, no timers. No apps in this category have peer-reviewed evidence; these come from practitioner guidance on design patterns.

Gap for Kalma: EndeavorRx is the only evidence-based app. It does not emphasize offline use or on-device AI. Other "calm" ADHD apps lack peer-reviewed evaluation.

#### Pakinig Mode: Dyslexia and Low Vision Apps

**Microsoft Immersive Reader** (free in Office 365, OneNote, Edge, and educational partner platforms). Features: read-aloud with word highlighting, syllable breaks, dyslexia-friendly fonts, adjustable colors and spacing, grammar overlays. Not a tutoring app; cannot be used standalone for mathematics. No peer-reviewed evidence of effectiveness. Based on accessibility best practices (W3C WCAG standards).

**ModMath** (iOS app ID 6740813075; free with $3.99/month ModMath Plus). Digital graph paper for students with dyslexia, dysgraphia, visual impairments, cerebral palsy. Custom keypads with math symbols, equation lining tools, supports arithmetic through quadratic equations. Not designed for read-aloud or audio-first learning. No peer-reviewed evidence found. Occupational therapy framework guides design.

**Khan Academy** (free online platform with screen reader support). Built-in read-aloud math feature requested by educators but not yet implemented. Educators report educator requests for math read-aloud functionality; platform acknowledges this in future planning but does not offer native read-aloud for mathematical notation.

Gap for Pakinig: Immersive Reader is read-aloud first but not a tutor. ModMath is visual and concrete but lacks audio. Khan Academy supports screen readers but not native read-aloud for math. No app combines tutor + audio-first + dyslexia accessibility.

#### Hakbang Mode: Dyscalculia and Intellectual Disability Apps

**Calcularis** (Constructor Technologies; Windows, Mac, iPad, Android; includes offline mode; $20/month subscription after free trial). Multisensory training for dyscalculia (ages 6+) using digits, blocks, and colors to target number sense.

Peer-reviewed evidence: Käser et al. (2013) published design and pilot evaluation in Frontiers in Psychology. Study design: intervention with children; measured impact on number representation and arithmetic operations using adaptive computational training. Specific quantitative improvements from peer-reviewed abstracts not detailed in publicly available metadata.

Published evidence: DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00489 (Frontiers in Psychology, 2013). [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00489)

Language: English confirmed. Offline: yes. One-step-per-reply model: not emphasized; self-paced learning.

**The Number Race** (web-based/downloadable game; open access). Designed for dyscalculia remediation based on the core number sense deficit hypothesis (Dehaene/Wilson framework).

Design paper published as: Wilson AJ, Dehaene S, Pinel P, Revkin SK, Cohen L, Cohen D. "Principles underlying the design of 'The Number Race', an adaptive computer game for remediation of dyscalculia." Behavioral and Brain Functions. DOI 10.1186/1744-9081-2-19 (2006).

This paper describes design with 9 children (ages 7-9) over 5 weeks of 30-minute sessions, 4 days/week. It is a design paper, not a controlled evaluation. Specific quantitative results reported in a companion article (noted in abstract). Language: English. Offline: downloadable. No evidence of peer-reviewed evaluation study published separately.

**onebillion onecourse** (delivered on low-cost onetabs; offline-first design; free via subsidized access in low-resource settings, paid licensing available). Comprehensive offline app for reading, writing, and numeracy. Deployed in resource-constrained contexts; solar-powered tablets possible.

Peer-reviewed evidence (verified):

1. Pitchford NJ, Papageorgiou KA, Outhwaite LA. "Development of early mathematical skills with a tablet intervention: a randomised control trial in Malawi." Frontiers in Psychology. DOI 10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00485 (2015). RCT design measuring mathematical skills via tablet-based assessment in early learners in Malawi. [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00485)

2. Outhwaite LA, Gulliford A, Pitchford NJ. "Closing the gap: Efficacy of a tablet intervention to support the development of early mathematical skills in UK primary school children." Computers & Education. DOI 10.1016/j.compedu.2017.01.011 (2017). RCT in UK primary schools measuring early mathematics development. [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131517300117)

Languages supported: Swahili, Yoruba, Lingala, and others; no Filipino documented. Offline: core design principle. One-step-per-reply model: not emphasized; adaptive self-paced learning.

**Gap for Hakbang:** Calcularis and onebillion are evidence-based for dyscalculia/numeracy but neither emphasizes "one step per reply" tutoring model. Both are self-paced. Hiyang's turn-by-turn tutor model with a check question per reply is not documented in existing apps.

#### Philippine Resources

**DepEd Learning Resources Management and Development System (LRMDS)** (lrmds.deped.gov.ph). Centralized portal for teaching guides, lesson plans, activity sheets, media assets. No dedicated neurodivergent or special education section documented. General K-12 and Alternative Learning System coverage.

**MATATAG Curriculum (DepEd)** (phased rollout starting SY 2024-2025 for K, Gr 1, 4, 7). Emphasizes foundational literacy, numeracy, and socio-emotional skills. No published special education differentiation for MATATAG documented as of October 2026.

**Gap:** No Philippine-developed neurodivergent tutoring app found. No evidence of Filipino/Taglish learning app for any of the three modes.

## Key Question 2: Research and Evidence Websites

### Takeaway

Primary research databases (ERIC, OpenAlex, Crossref) offer free access to education research. Specialized evidence syntheses (IES What Works Clearinghouse, EEF Teaching and Learning Toolkit, NCII) rank interventions by impact. Design guidance (CAST UDL 3.0, WCAG 2.2) provides accessibility frameworks. Philippine resources (ejournals.ph, DepEd LRMDS) lack centralized neurodivergent focus.

### Research Databases

**ERIC (Educational Resources Information Center)**
Largest education database: 1.5+ million records of journal articles, research reports, dissertations, conference papers. Sponsored by U.S. Department of Education. Free access via ERIC.ed.gov. Specialized vocabulary for special education ("Students with Disabilities"). Boolean search: "learning disabilities AND computer assisted instruction AND mathematics". Best for: education research on neurodivergent learners and intervention efficacy aligned to US frameworks (IDEA, RTI/MTSS). Example search: [ERIC special education math learning disabilities](https://guides.libraries.psu.edu/sped/articles)

**OpenAlex** (formerly part of larger scholarly ecosystem)
Free, open bibliographic database: 321 million works, 120 million authors, 283,000 journals/repos (July 2026). Alternative to Web of Science and Scopus. As of February 2026, requires API keys and usage-based pricing (shift from free model). Best for: broad bibliometric analysis and discovery across education technology and learning sciences fields. API access: https://api.openalex.org/works?search=TERMS&per_page=25

**Crossref**
Global scholarly metadata registry. Free API access to DOI resolution and citation data. Essential for verifying publication details, authors, journal, and publication year before citing research. API: https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=TERMS&rows=20. Best for: confirming paper authenticity and checking journal credibility.

**Semantic Scholar**
Free academic search engine with NLP-powered paper summaries and citation links. Covers computer science, education, and learning sciences. Best for: quick discovery of papers with AI-generated abstracts.

#### Evidence Syntheses and Practice Guides

**IES What Works Clearinghouse (WWC)** (Institute of Education Sciences, U.S. Department of Education)
Reviews education programs, products, practices against rigorous evidence standards. 400+ intervention reports; "Find What Works" tool filters by subject, grade, and student population. Evidence ratings: positive effect, potentially positive, mixed, no discernible effect, negative effect (based on RCT and quasi-experimental studies). Includes special education math intervention reviews. Free access: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/ Best for: evidence-based practices with quality ratings and implementation guidance.

**Education Endowment Foundation (EEF) Teaching and Learning Toolkit** (UK-based, international reach)
Free resource summarizing 30+ teaching strategies ranked by impact, cost, and evidence strength. Synthesizes 250+ meta-analyses and systematic reviews. Updated annually (May 2025 commitment to "living systematic reviews"). Covers metacognition, oral language, reading comprehension, mathematical reasoning, early numeracy, phonics, writing. Access: https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit Best for: practical evidence-based strategies and cost-effectiveness guidance for school leaders.

**NCII (National Center on Intensive Intervention)** (intensiveintervention.org; U.S. Department of Education OSEP)
Technical assistance center for intensive intervention in reading, mathematics, behavior. Curates and reviews commercial math intervention programs. Develops math lessons aligned to college/career-ready standards for students with math learning disabilities. Organized by six math skill areas. Free resources, webinars, downloadable guides. Best for: vetted math intervention programs and instructional guidance for students with dyscalculia and math disabilities.

#### Accessibility and Design Guidance

**CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0** (Center for Applied Special Technology; released 2024)
Framework for accessible curriculum design with three principles: Multiple Means of Engagement, Representation, and Action/Expression. Aligned to neuroscience of learning. 9 core design checkpoints with examples for educational technology. Free access: https://udlguidelines.cast.org Best for: designing inclusive learning experiences for neurodivergent and diverse learners; directly informs accessible tutor design.

**W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2** (World Wide Web Consortium; published October 5, 2023)
International standard for web accessibility: 86 success criteria across 4 principles (Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, Robust). New for WCAG 2.2: 9 success criteria focused on cognitive and learning disabilities, low vision, and mobile accessibility. Covers readability (font sizes, color contrast, spacing), navigation clarity, predictability, input error prevention. Free: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/ Best for: ensuring app interfaces are accessible to users with cognitive disabilities, dyslexia, low vision, and motor impairments.

#### Philippine and Global South Resources

**Philippine E-Journals** (ejournals.ph)
Open-access peer-reviewed articles from Philippine universities and research institutions. Includes special education and SPED practitioner research. Subject coverage: education, psychology, development, social sciences. Free full-text access. Best for: accessing practitioner research on Philippine special education; finding culturally relevant classroom research.

**DepEd Learning Resources Management and Development System (LRMDS)** (lrmds.deped.gov.ph)
Centralized portal for teaching and learning resources in Philippines. Includes lesson plans, daily lesson logs, activity sheets, media assets, textbooks, professional development. No dedicated neurodivergent learner section documented. Free after registration. Best for: accessing MATATAG-aligned curriculum materials; lacks neurodivergent differentiation as of October 2026.

**Kolibri** (open-source LMS; github.com/learningequality/kolibri)
Offline content delivery system for low-resource and low-connectivity contexts. Supports 173+ languages; curated K-12 content from Khan Academy, OpenStax, CK-12. Deployed in 200+ countries, refugee camps, orphanages, non-formal education. Best for: understanding offline educational infrastructure relevant to Hiyang's offline-first design philosophy.

## Gaps and Unique Position

### Gaps in Current Ecosystem

1. No app combines: offline-first, on-device AI tutor, mode-specific pedagogies (Kalma/Pakinig/Hakbang), and Filipino language.

2. ADHD/autism (Kalma) apps: EndeavorRx is sole evidence-based app; it is online and not designed for offline use.

3. Dyslexia/low vision (Pakinig): Immersive Reader and ModMath exist separately but neither integrates tutoring with read-aloud math.

4. Dyscalculia (Hakbang): Calcularis and onebillion are evidence-based but neither emphasizes turn-by-turn "one step per reply" tutoring.

5. "One step per reply" pedagogy: No app in literature emphasizes tight tutor-student turn-taking with a check question per reply. This pedagogical model (unique to Hiyang) has no existing benchmark.

6. Filipino/Taglish support: All evidence-based math apps (Calcularis, The Number Race, onebillion, EndeavorRx, Khan Academy) are English-only or regional-language. onebillion supports Swahili and Yoruba but not Filipino.

7. Philippine app ecosystem: No Philippine-developed neurodivergent tutoring app found in research. MATATAG curriculum lacks published special education differentiation.

### Hiyang's Claimed Position

1. Offline-first and on-device AI tutor: no competitor combines both.
2. Three mode-specific pedagogies (Kalma calm, Pakinig audio, Hakbang concrete) in one system: competitors use single-mode design.
3. Filipino/Taglish support: unmatched in evidence-based math tutoring.
4. MATATAG Grades 1-6 alignment: proprietary to Philippine context.
5. Turn-by-turn scaffolding model: no app in literature follows this design.
