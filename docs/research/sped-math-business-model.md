# Pakinig Sustains Through Institutional Funding, Not Learners

**BLUF: The viable business model for Pakinig is government institutional procurement (DepEd, LGU, DSWD) combined with telco data subsidies and CSR tax incentives, not direct payment from blind learners or their families. The Philippine market expects free assistive tech for blind/low-vision children; global precedent (Bookshare, Be My Eyes, Seeing AI, NVDA) is unanimous. Pakinig's offline design fits the 47,000 public schools without internet and the roughly 4.7 million children with disabilities not enrolled in public school (EDCOM 2 estimate). The realistic first step is a funded pilot of a few hundred learners, then division-level rollouts paid through existing channels (DepEd PHP 850M SPED budget, the LGU Special Education Fund, corporate Adopt-a-School donations).**

---

## The Market: 5.1 Million Children with Disabilities, 92 Percent Out of Reach

An estimated 5.1 million Filipino children have disabilities, but only 391,089 (8 percent) were enrolled in public schools in the 2024-2025 school year, leaving **4.7 million children out of school or under-resourced** ([EDCOM 2 Study, June 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)). Among learners with disabilities already in schools, 61 percent lack formal medical diagnosis and are categorized only by observable symptoms, suggesting widespread misidentification and underservice even for those enrolled ([DepEd LIS data, 2025](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)). Visual impairment is a substantial component: 57 percent of individuals with severe disability aged 15 and older experience vision problems ([Philippine Statistics Authority National Disability Prevalence Survey, 2016](https://psa.gov.ph/statistics/national-disability-prevalence-survey)). This figure covers adults aged 15 and over, not children, and the research found no published count of blind and low-vision children or of visually impaired learners in Grades 4-6. Do not quote a child market size in the pitch; say instead that no national count exists, which is itself part of the problem. **The infrastructure gap is severe: only one national school for the blind (PNSB) with 144 students operates in the entire country, and 60 percent of learners with disabilities have zero access to any Special Education (SPED) centers, resource centers, or trained teachers** ([DepEd/IDinsight, EDCOM 2, 2026](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/edcom-2-study-warns-5m-filipino-children-with-disabilities-still-underserve/)).

## Why Learners Cannot Pay: The Economics of Disability and Free App Preference

Filipino families with members with disabilities are predominantly low-income, and the market has demonstrated overwhelming preference for free educational applications. Household willingness to pay for education services is low: Philippine education apps market is dominated by free or freemium models (YouTube, Facebook, Brainly, Khan Academy, Edukasyon.ph), and **the market reached only PHP 124 million in 2024, with free platforms capturing the majority of usage** ([IMARC Group, 2024](https://www.imarcgroup.com/philippines-education-apps-market)). The average Filipino household spends PHP 1,281 per month on internet connection, which is prohibitive for families below the poverty line (approximately PHP 4,200 per month income), representing 25-30 percent of discretionary spending for very low-income households ([PIDS research](https://www.pids.gov.ph/details/the-great-divide/)).

Government provides limited income support: RA 10754 grants persons with disabilities a 20 percent discount on goods and services and VAT exemption on medical and educational support, but these are not universal and require PWD ID registration and active claim processes. Families do not reliably access these benefits ([RA 10754, multiple sources](https://jur.ph/law/summary/expanding-benefits-for-persons-with-disability/)). **Global assistive technology precedent is decisive: all major platforms serving blind and low-vision users are free to learners.** Be My Eyes (6 million volunteers, free to users; revenue from corporate B2B partnerships). Bookshare (free to K-12 students, funded by USD 42.5 million US Department of Education contract). NVDA (free open-source screen reader, funding from donations). Microsoft Seeing AI (free mobile app, backed by Microsoft's USD 25 million AI for Accessibility program). None of these charge end users; revenue sources are government contracts, corporate partnerships, or donations ([Be My Eyes revenue model](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/be-my-eyes-a-heartwarming-6-million-volunteers-assisting-blind-low-vision-users-d593c8248d); [Benetech/Bookshare DOE contract](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/palo-altos-benetech-wins-42-5m-dept-of-education-contract-to-help-the-blind-read/); [NV Access nonprofit profile](https://causes.benevity.org/causes/036-96149271036); [Microsoft AI for Accessibility](https://www.slashgear.com/microsoft-ai-for-accessibility-is-a-25m-fund-for-smart-disability-tech-07529874/)). **The user business model for Pakinig must be free; no B2C subscription pricing exists for blind/low-vision assistive education tech globally, and attempting to charge Filipino families would contradict both market expectation and the disability service precedent.**

## Government Funding is Fragmented but Present: Three Revenue Pathways

### DepEd SPED Budget and Institutional Partnerships

DepEd has dedicated funding for special education that has grown significantly: PHP 581.6 million in 2023 (after initially zero allocation), expanded to **PHP 850 million in 2025 with an additional PHP 541.5 million requested to support teacher training and services across 30,000 public schools** ([Inquirer News](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1986877/deped-thanks-house-ok-on-proposed-p793-1-billion-budget-for-2025)). However, DepEd does not separately itemize software, apps, or digital learning tools in published budget documents; schools must source these through Maintenance and Other Operating Expenses (MOOE), the Special Education Fund (SEF), or partnerships with private companies. **DepEd's acquisition preference is Memoranda of Agreement (MOAs) with edtech companies for free or deeply subsidized access, not traditional procurement.** Evidence: Khan Academy (MOA signed August 2024, free to learners nationwide, funded by Smart telecommunications data subsidy plus Ayala Foundation partnership, reaching 711,000 learners and 12,153 teachers across 2,700+ public schools by December 2024) ([DepEd Khan Academy partnership](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2024/12/12/deped-partners-with-khan-academy-ph-smart-to-bring-digital-transformation-closer-to-schools-learners/); [Ayala Group partnership](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/12/22/2408981/ayala-group-teams-deped-khan-academy)); Frontlearners (MOA signed August 2025 to roll out AI-powered learning to 500+ schools, no pricing model disclosed, appears free or subsidized); Canva (partnership October 2024 for free Canva for Education access). **Pakinig's pathway: negotiate an MOA with DepEd for free access to learners, with funding underwritten by government allocation plus corporate co-investment or a nonprofit partner's grant (e.g., foundation grant to manage deployment and teacher training).**

### LGU Special Education Fund: Decentralized, Now Open to Learners with Disabilities

Every province, city and municipality levies an additional 1 percent real property tax for its Special Education Fund (SEF), managed by the Local School Board. Despite its name, the SEF funds public schools in general, not special education specifically, and no fixed share of it is reserved for learners with disabilities. SEF size varies widely by LGU because it tracks local real property values. DepEd-DBM-DILG-DOF Joint Circular No. 01, s. 2025 issued omnibus guidelines on SEF use and expanded the learners it covers to include learners with disabilities, out-of-school and adult learners, which gives a legal basis to fund supports for learners with disabilities; the research did not confirm whether it names apps or software as allowable expenses ([Joint Circular No. 01, s. 2025](https://www.dbm.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Issuances/2025/Joint-Circular/DEPED-DBM-DILG-DOF-JOINT-CIRCULAR-NO.-01,-s.-2025.pdf)). **Practical implication: A school or school division can purchase Pakinig licenses using SEF allocation without central DepEd procurement, but requires local advocacy and school board buy-in. This is a secondary revenue pathway, not primary.**

### Assistive Technology Programs (DSWD, PhilHealth, NCDA)

The Department of Social Welfare and Development operates a Comprehensive Program for Persons with Disabilities providing assistive devices (talking laptops, braille systems, hearing aids, smart canes, motorized wheelchairs), and the National Council on Disability Affairs coordinates inter-agency provision of assistive technology ([NCDA guidelines](https://ncda.gov.ph/?p=13084); the DSWD program details were not confirmed against a DSWD source). PhilHealth provides Z Benefits for visual, hearing, mobility, and neuro-developmental disabilities in children 18 and under, covering physical assistive devices ranging from PHP 12,730 to PHP 163,540 (prosthetics, orthotics, bracing). **However, no data shows DSWD or PhilHealth covering educational software for learners with disabilities.** PhilHealth's coverage is limited to physical devices, and DSWD's assistive tech program appears focused on employment and mobility aids, not learning apps. The National School for the Blind has received donation of assistive devices (25 OrCam MyEye 2 devices from St. Joseph and Aboitiz Foundations in 2019) but state budget allocations for assistive technology or educational software are not published ([OrCam blog](https://www.orcam.com/en-us/blog/the-aboitiz-foundation-and-st-joseph-foundation-donate-25-orcam-myeye-2-devices)). **Implication: DSWD and PhilHealth are not direct revenue sources for Pakinig; however, PNSB, school divisions, and disability organizations may have discretionary budgets or CSR partnerships that can fund deployment.**

## RA 11650 and ILRC Rollout: New Procurement Window Opening

Republic Act 11650 (Instituting a Policy of Inclusion and Services for Learners with Disabilities) was signed into law in March 2022, with Implementing Rules and Regulations issued in December 2024, creating an operational governance framework for inclusive education. Critical operational guidelines remain pending as of October 2026, including the comprehensive Individualized Education Program (IEP) framework, assessment tools, alternative educational programs, early intervention, inter-agency referral systems, and **private sector participation** ([DepEd RA 11650 IRR](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2025/03/03/implementing-rules-and-regulations-of-republic-act-no-11650-otherwise-known-as-the-instituting-a-policy-of-inclusion-and-services-for-learners-with-disabilities-in-support-of-inclusive-education-a/)). The government announced in April 2025 that it is preparing to roll out AI-driven initiatives to support learners with disabilities, including advanced Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs) with virtual and satellite versions to broaden access to specialized resources, with pilot testing set for select Metro Manila schools ([Cebu Daily News](https://cebudailynews.inquirer.net/635104/deped-to-roll-out-ai-driven-programs-for-learners-with-disabilities)). A previous proposal sought to increase DepEd's capital outlay for SPED from PHP 96 million to PHP 160 million to ensure all regions would have a model ILRC ([The Post](https://thepost.net.ph/news/nation/lawmaker-bats-for-inclusive-learning-resource-center-pushes-for-full-implementation-of-ra-11650/)). **This creates a significant but uncertain procurement opportunity: if ILRCs are equipped with educational technology, Pakinig could be pitched as a core learning tool, but guidelines on private sector participation and procurement processes are not yet finalized.** Pakinig should prepare for ILRC adoption by demonstrating RA 11650 compliance and ready integration with offline, accessible learning environments.

## Local AI and Offline Economics: Operational Advantage, Not Consumer Pricing Lever

Pakinig's offline-first design using Whisper speech recognition and local AI inference (no cloud dependency) provides real operational advantages: zero per-learner cloud inference cost, enabling deployment to the 47,000 DepEd schools without internet access ([DepEd statements, 2024-2025](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/2080790/deped-to-connect-all-public-schools-to-the-internet-within-2025/)), and sustainable operation in rural areas where connectivity is unreliable or expensive (Muslim Mindanao internet access at only 4 percent, compared to 66 percent in Metro Manila) ([PIDS analysis](https://www.pids.gov.ph/details/the-great-divide/)). However, **this cost advantage does not translate into lower end-user pricing.** Kolibri (offline learning platform serving low-income primary school children in internet-constrained regions, funded by USD 5 million Google.org grant plus USD 250,000 crowdfunding over 3 years) and OneBillion (offline adaptive literacy and numeracy app for onetab low-cost device, funded by UK Aid and Comic Relief) remain free despite zero per-learner cloud costs ([Learning Equality Kolibri crowdfunding](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/kolibri_crowdfunding_campaign_flies_past_goal_reaching_203_percent); [EdTech Hub Malawi onebillion scaling](https://edtechhub.org/?p=15024)). **Cost savings accrue to the provider (lower operational cost per child served) and funder (more children served per dollar), not end users.** Pakinig's offline capability is operationally essential for Philippine reach but commercially invisible; it reduces the cost of serving unconnected schools and low-bandwidth learners, improving the sustainability of institutional funding models, but does not justify direct monetization.

## Telco Data Subsidies: Khan Academy Model Scaled to Pakinig

Khan Academy's December 2024 DepEd partnership demonstrates a high-leverage funding mechanism available to Pakinig: **Smart Communications provides free data access (5GB weekly for prepaid subscribers, 10GB for postpaid) to Khan Academy content through DepEd Commons integration, removing the data cost barrier for learners while subsidizing the app's distribution at scale** ([DepEd Khan Academy partnership](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2024/12/12/deped-partners-with-khan-academy-ph-smart-to-bring-digital-transformation-closer-to-schools-learners/); [Ayala Group partnership](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/12/22/2408981/ayala-group-teams-deped-khan-academy)). This model is replicable for Pakinig: negotiate with Smart, Globe, or DITO (Philippine telecommunications providers) to zero-rate Pakinig's data access on their network, funded by telco marketing budgets or government Universal Service Fund allocations. The telco gains branding and CSR credit; learners gain free access; DepEd gains a scaled learning tool; Pakinig gains user acquisition and sustainability. **This pathway requires: (1) a working DepEd partnership to establish credibility, (2) willingness by telco to co-invest in CSR, and (3) scale (50,000+ learners) to justify telco marketing spend.**

## CSR and Tax Incentives: RA 8525 and Corporate Sponsorship

RA 8525 (Adopt-a-School Program Act) lets a company that adopts a public school deduct its program expenses and claim an additional deduction from gross income equal to 50 percent of those expenses (150 percent in total). Covered assistance explicitly includes "books, publications and other instructional materials" and "modernization of instructional technologies", which fits a donated learning app plus devices and teacher training ([RA 8525](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1998/ra_8525_1998.html); [BIR Revenue Regulation 013-2025](https://serp-p.pids.gov.ph/publication/public/view?slug=bir-issues-policy-on-grant-of-tax-perks-under-ebet-adopt-school-programs); [DepEd Adopt-a-School portal](https://www.deped.gov.ph/about-adopt-a-school/)). A corporate sponsor could donate Pakinig installation and teacher training to a school or school division, claim the tax deduction, and report measurable CSR impact (students served, learning outcomes). This is attractive for large companies with high net income seeking ESG/CSR credit. **Practical pathway: Identify 2-3 corporate sponsors (SM Foundation's disability focus, Ayala Foundation's education programs, Globe Foundation) and structure Pakinig deployment as a school donation under RA 8525, with corporate tax credit and ESG reporting.** This is a complement to government funding, not a primary revenue source, as corporate interest in specific disability education apps is limited and requires ongoing cultivation.

## Government Procurement Rules: RA 12009 Simplifies Small-Scale Purchase

The New Government Procurement Act (RA 12009, effective 2024) replaces RA 9184 and expands procurement flexibility by allowing Small Value Procurement (SVP) up to PHP 2,000,000 with at least three price quotations, direct contracting, and negotiated procurement modes, all with reduced documentation compared to open bidding ([GPPB Small Value Procurement](https://www.gppb.gov.ph/?p=12337)). A school division can now purchase Pakinig licenses directly (without central DepEd procurement) via SVP or negotiated procurement if justification is provided for a specialized product (e.g., assistive technology tailored to blind learners). However, **no documented case examples of schools using direct contracting or SVP for educational software exist in public records,** making this pathway theoretical. DepEd's observed preference is MOAs with free or subsidy-enabled access (Khan Academy, Frontlearners, Canva), not traditional licensing procurement. **Implication: SVP and direct contracting are possible but unproven pathways; focus on MOA partnerships with DepEd as primary procurement route.**

## Government Startup Grants: DICT, DOST, PCIEERD

Three Philippine government programs fund educational technology startups without equity loss:

1. **DICT Startup Grant Fund (Cohort 2 closed, Cohort 3 dates TBD):** PHP 500,000 to PHP 1,000,000 equity-free grants for startups in proof-of-concept to MVP stage ([DICT Startup Grant Fund FAQ](https://startup.gov.ph/dict-startup-grant-fund-frequently-asked-questions/)). Eligibility requires DTI or SEC startup registration (1-7 years old) and working prototype.

2. **DOST Startup Grant Fund:** Up to PHP 5 million per startup. The 2026 call found was run by DOST-PCAARRD, the council for agriculture and aquatic startups, so it is not the right door for edtech ([DOST-PCAARRD 2026 call](https://www.pcaarrd.dost.gov.ph/index.php/quick-information-dispatch-qid-articles/dost-pcaarrd-opens-call-for-2026-startup-grant-fund-program)).

3. **PCIEERD Startup Grant Fund:** Names Education Technologies as a focus area; no published cap. The 2026 call closed on 31 July 2026, so target the next call ([PCIEERD call, via a grant aggregator](https://fundsforcompanies.fundsforngos.org/grant/call-for-proposals-pcieerd-startup-grant-fund-program-philippines/)).

**Pakinig is likely eligible for DICT SGF Cohort 3 once it is registered as a startup with a working prototype, and for the next PCIEERD call if the roadmap shows scaling to multiple schools.** These grants are valuable for proof-of-concept and pilot funding but insufficient for scaled deployment; they bridge the gap between development and government/corporate partnership.

## International Assistive Technology Funding: AT2030 as Accessible Entry Point

**AT2030 (UK Aid-funded, led by Global Disability Innovation Hub) is the most accessible international funding source for assistive education technology in the Philippines.** Operates in 40+ countries including the Philippines; AT Impact Fund provides flexible grants and venture-building support for assistive tech startups ([AT2030 AT Impact Fund](https://at2030.org/at-impact-fund/); [AT2030 Philippines work](https://at2030.org/static/at2030_core/outputs/Global-Developments-in-Assistive-Technology-B-21.pdf)). AT2030 is actively working on financing mechanisms for assistive technologies through PhilHealth. UNICEF Venture Fund (2024 Annual Report confirms AI for Accessibility as core impact area) likely accepts applications from Philippine social enterprises but details on 2025-2026 calls not confirmed ([UNICEF Venture Fund AR 2024](https://www.unicefventurefund.org/sites/default/files/2025-07/AR%202024%20-%20External.pdf)). Zero Project (Austrian-led, Call for Nominations 2027) awards recognition for innovations in assistive technology globally, not direct funding, but recognition could unlock secondary sponsorship ([Zero Project Awards 2027](https://zeroproject.org/)). Google.org historical precedent (USD 250,000 grant to INCO and AIJC in 2020 for educator capacity building in distance learning) suggests openness to pedagogy-focused projects ([Google.org PH](https://mb.com.ph/2020/08/24/google-delivers-four-initiatives-to-support-inclusive-distance-learning-in-the-philippines/)), but no 2025-2026 edtech calls are confirmed. **AT2030 is the most actionable international pathway; Microsoft AI for Accessibility (USD 25 million fund mentioned in research but no confirmed 2025-2026 calls for Philippines) and Google.org are lower-probability.**

## Conclusion: Recommended Business Model and Phased Path

**Pakinig's sustainable business model combines government institutional funding (primary), telco data subsidies (secondary), and CSR tax incentives (tertiary), with free access to learners and teachers.** No B2C subscription model is viable; the precedent across all global blind/low-vision assistive technology is free to users, funded by government contracts, corporate partnerships, or donations.

### Recommended Model

**Who pays:** DepEd (SPED budget), LGU SEF, telco (Smart/Globe data subsidy), corporate CSR sponsors via RA 8525, and government startup grants.

**For what:** Free offline app access for learners and teachers; deployment (preloaded devices, setup), teacher training, and content updates aligned to the DepEd MATATAG curriculum. There is no per-learner server bill, because speech recognition and answer checking run on the device.

**At what price:** Free to learners. Institutions pay for a school or division package (deployment, training, support) at cost recovery; a per-learner figure such as PHP 50-200 per year is a planning assumption, not a benchmark the research found. Corporate sponsors fund packages as Adopt-a-School donations. Startup grants fund the pilot (one-time, PHP 500K to PHP 5M).

### Phased Path to Revenue and Scale

**Phase 1: Pilot and Proof (October 2026 to March 2027)**
- Secure one government startup grant: DICT SGF Cohort 3 (PHP 500K-1M) or the next PCIEERD call (the 2026 call closed 31 July 2026).
- Partner with PNSB or one major school division (Metro Manila or Cebu) for pilot deployment to 200-500 learners.
- Iterate product based on blind learner feedback and teacher input.
- Measure and document outcomes: completion rates, grade improvement, accessibility metrics.

**Phase 2: First Institutional Adoption (April to September 2027)**
- Negotiate MOA with DepEd (or specific school division) for free access to learners, with funding from (a) DepEd SPED allocation, (b) corporate co-investment via RA 8525 Adopt-a-School donation, or (c) foundation grant (Ayala, GiveWell-aligned organizations).
- Target 5,000 to 15,000 learners across 50-100 schools.
- Establish teacher training program and support infrastructure.
- Approach Smart, Globe, or DITO for zero-rated data partnership discussion (milestone-based).

**Phase 3: Scale Through Government Programs (October 2027 onward)**
- Expand DepEd partnership to ILRC rollout (as guidelines finalize and procurement opens).
- Activate LGU SEF purchases through school divisions (pilot in 3-5 cities with high SEF allocation and disability advocacy).
- Launch telco data subsidy partnership with one major telco (Smart or Globe), targeting 50,000+ learners.
- Maintain corporate sponsor pipeline via RA 8525 and SM Foundation, Ayala Foundation engagement.
- Explore AT2030 or UNICEF Venture Fund support for scaling to second/third regions.

## Addressing Likely Judge Questions

**Q: Who pays if families can't afford a subscription?**
A: Families don't. Pakinig is free to users. Government (DepEd, LGU) pays through existing SPED allocations; telcos pay through marketing/CSR budgets; corporations pay through RA 8525 tax-deductible donations. This is the global precedent for blind/low-vision assistive education (Bookshare, Seeing AI, NVDA are all free to users).

**Q: Why not just free like Khan Academy?**
A: Pakinig IS free like Khan Academy, with the same revenue model. Khan Academy succeeds through a DepEd MOA plus Smart data subsidy plus Ayala Foundation partnership. Pakinig's differentiation is offline-first design (critical for 47,000 unconnected schools) and voice-only interface (optimized for blind learners). This is not a "why not free" question but a "how is it sustainable" question. Answer: DepEd SPED budget (PHP 850M in 2025), the LGU Special Education Fund (whose 2025 guidelines now cover learners with disabilities), telco partnerships, and corporate CSR, the same mix that carries Khan Academy in DepEd schools. Khan Academy itself has no offline, voice-only path for blind learners, which is the gap Pakinig fills.

**Q: What does local AI (Whisper offline) change about the economics?**
A: Local AI removes the per-learner cloud inference cost, so the subsidy needed per learner is lower and the app keeps working in offline regions (47,000 schools without internet). However, this operational advantage doesn't reduce end-user price (remains free) but increases funder return (more learners served per peso of government budget). Offline capability is operationally essential for Philippine market reach but commercially invisible to pricing.

**Q: Why should DepEd fund Pakinig instead of just training more SPED teachers?**
A: It should do both. Philippines has a 1:63 teacher-to-learner ratio for SPED; teacher training is 10+ year recruitment and development pipeline. Pakinig addresses the gap NOW: offline app supporting self-paced learning and teacher instruction, reaching 60% of learners with disabilities who have zero access to any SPED service. An app can be copied to every school for almost nothing once it works; teacher recruitment cannot scale that fast. App plus teachers is the realistic strategy, not either/or.
