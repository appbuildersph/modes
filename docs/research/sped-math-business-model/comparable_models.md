# Business Models for Offline EdTech Serving Learners with Disabilities

Research on comparable products and their revenue structures to inform Pakinig's (offline Progressive Web App for blind/low-vision Grade 4-6 math learners in the Philippines) business model decision. Current as of October 2026.

---

## Philippine EdTech Monetization Models

### Takeaway

Philippine edtech platforms use a mix of B2C subscriptions, government procurement contracts, telco data partnerships, and CSR-funded free tiers. Quipper shows sustainable B2C pricing (300-3,600 PHP annually); Khan Academy's DepEd partnership demonstrates that free tier models work when funded by telecoms (Smart data subsidy) or institutional partners; DepEd procurement is active but subject to repeat-vendor consolidation and split-contract concerns.

### Cited Findings

- **Quipper School (DepEd-aligned K12)**: Free tier exists; **Quipper Video (grades 10-12)**: 300 PHP/month or 3,600 PHP/year (may vary by contract). Company operates in Japan, Indonesia, Philippines, Mexico. — [Quipper pricing overview](https://subger.com/en/service/quipper-id); [Quipper company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quipper_(company))

- **Khan Academy Philippines (DepEd partnership, Dec 2024)**: Free platform to learners; funded by telco (Smart Communications provides free data: 5GB weekly for prepaid, 10GB postpaid subscribers); Ayala Foundation, Globe Telecom, and iPeople provide institutional support. Reached 711,000 learner-beneficiaries and 12,153 teachers across 2,700+ public schools as of partnership announcement. — [DepEd Khan Academy partnership](https://www.deped.gov.ph/2024/12/12/deped-partners-with-khan-academy-ph-smart-to-bring-digital-transformation-closer-to-schools-learners/); [Ayala Group DepEd Khan Academy](https://www.philstar.com/business/2024/12/22/2408981/ayala-group-teams-deped-khan-academy)

- **DepEd Government Procurement (2024-2025)**: DepEd procured 1 million Google Workspace licenses for 900,000+ teachers; awarded procurement of LinkedIn Learning subscriptions via Virentis Search Asia; conducted pilot of low-cost tablets connected to local area network with solar antenna for distance learning. Procurement process accelerated in 2025; suppliers.deped.gov.ph registry launched to increase transparency. Concerns raised about vendor concentration: five companies reported dominating billions of pesos in DepEd computer supply contracts. — [DepEd Google Workspace procurement](https://manilatimes.net/2025/03/10/videos/watch-deped-procures-one-million-google-workspace/2070245); [DepEd procurement gains 2024](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/?p=2167997); [DepEd vendor concentration concerns](https://newsinfo.inquirer.net/1667159/jinggoy-5-firms-get-juicy-deped-deals)

### Inferences

- **B2C subscription works in Philippines at modest price points**: 300 PHP/month (~$5-6 USD) is accessible to middle-class Filipino families; annual upfront payment (3,600 PHP) reduces friction for affordability-conscious buyers.
- **Telco data subsidy is a powerful DepEd-scale lever**: Khan Academy's reach (711K users) leveraged Smart's existing postpaid/prepaid customer base and data infrastructure. For math apps, partnering with Smart, Globe, or DITO could subsidize data costs at scale.
- **DepEd procurement is reliable but consolidates around repeat vendors**: Established relationships and repeat orders favor companies already in government supply chains. New entrants face bid competition but contracts are substantial.
- **CSR and foundation funding de-risk launch in DepEd**: Khan Academy, Google, Ayala Foundation partnership model shows that institutional co-investment can underwrite free access at large scale.

### Gaps

- **Specific pricing for local Filipino edtech apps** (math-focused, MATATAG-aligned, not Khan Academy): No data found on local competitors' B2C prices; unclear if 300-3,600 PHP pricing is optimal for SPED-focused products or if lower pricing is needed.
- **Failure or shutdown data for Philippine edtech platforms**: No information found on apps that discontinued or pivoted; unclear what price points or business models failed in PH market.
- **DepEd procurement timeline and budget for math/SPED products**: No public budget figures or RFQ cycles found; unclear how large DepEd math app contracts are or when next procurement window opens.

## Accessibility and Assistive Technology Revenue Models (Global and Southeast Asia)

### Takeaway

Free-to-user assistive tech for blind/low-vision learners is sustainable across diverse models: B2B enterprise services (Be My Eyes, Microsoft), government contracts with education quotas (Benetech/Bookshare, American Printing House), and nonprofit grant/donation funding (NVDA/NV Access). User data never monetized; revenue from employers, institutions, or public funding. No assistive tech for blind learners found in SE Asia with published revenue.

### Cited Findings

**Be My Eyes (Global, free mobile app)**
- **Business model**: Free to visually impaired users and sighted volunteers; revenue from B2B enterprise services (corporate volunteering, specialized help, Be My Eyes for Work, careers matching, experiences/events).
- **Key partnerships for revenue**: Microsoft, Google, Lloyds Banking Group, Procter & Gamble provide expert video support via "Specialized Help" feature; employees use platform as volunteering/CSR.
- **Funding history**: Raised $2.8 million in venture funding; operates with "purpose and profit" model keeping user-facing service free and unlimited.
— [Be My Eyes revenue model](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/be-my-eyes-a-heartwarming-6-million-volunteers-assisting-blind-low-vision-users-d593c8248d); [Be My Eyes partnerships TechCrunch](https://invisionmag.com/be-my-eyes-raises-2-8-million-in-funding-for-app-to-support-blind-and-visually-impaired/)

**Benetech / Bookshare (USA nonprofit, 501(c)(3))**
- **Funding mix** (2017): 80% from operating revenue (Bookshare subscriptions and services), 20% from donor/foundation support. Annual revenue $13.4M.
- **Government contracts**: US Department of Education awarded $42.5M five-year contract to expand accessible e-book library (third time renewed). Serves over 1.5 million learners; 775,000+ books in 47 languages.
- **User pricing**: Students get free Bookshare access (underwritten by US Dept of Education); adults pay $75/year for individual subscription.
- **Risk capital model**: Benetech projects took $9M in nonprofit risk capital and attracted $107M in follow-on grants/revenue.
— [Benetech TechCrunch DOE contract](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/01/palo-altos-benetech-wins-42-5m-dept-of-education-contract-to-help-the-blind-read/); [Benetech revenue model NPQ](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/2017/11/03/behind-benetech-model-applying-time-money-tech-social-benefit/); [Bookshare About](https://www.bookshare.org/cms/about)

**American Printing House for the Blind (APH, USA, 501(c)(3))**
- **Federal Quota Program**: Congress appropriates annual funds; per-student allocation is divided by eligible blind/low-vision K-12 enrollment (census on first Monday of January). Schools and state departments use quota funds to purchase educational materials from APH.
- **Revenue**: $22.6 million annual (2020).
- **Coverage**: Federal quota funds serve blind/low-vision students in public schools, schools for the blind, rehabilitation programs, multiple disabilities programs across all 50 states.
— [APH Federal Quota](https://aph.org/federal-quota); [APH Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Printing_House_for_the_Blind)

**NVDA / NV Access (Australia, nonprofit)**
- **Business model**: Free open-source screen reader funded by grants and donations.
- **Funding**: Donations and foundation grants support developers, hosting, tools, conference attendance.
- **No monetization**: NVDA provided free worldwide; funded entirely by donations to NV Access Limited.
— [NV Access Benevity nonprofit profile](https://causes.benevity.org/causes/036-96149271036)

**Microsoft Seeing AI and Microsoft AI for Accessibility (USA)**
- **Model**: Seeing AI is free mobile app for visually impaired users; backed by Microsoft's corporate accessibility investment.
- **Funding**: $25 million, five-year Microsoft AI for Accessibility program providing seed grants to developers, universities, institutions; scaling promising projects; incorporating wins into Microsoft cloud services.
- **Enterprise component**: Microsoft partner businesses eligible for funding and technical support to integrate AI accessibility features.
- **Corporate funding**: Venture-backed innovation funded directly by Microsoft; not user-revenue dependent.
— [Microsoft AI for Accessibility $25M](https://www.slashgear.com/microsoft-ai-for-accessibility-is-a-25m-fund-for-smart-disability-tech-07529874/); [Microsoft disability investment](https://www.techspot.com/community/topics/microsoft-initiative-puts-ai-to-work-for-people-with-disabilities.246448/)

**Southeast Asia Disability EdTech (Limited Data)**
- **Research found**: EdTech Hub conducted Rapid Evidence Reviews on EdTech for learners with cognitive/learning disabilities and hearing/visual impairments in SE Asia (Brunei, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, Timor-Leste, Vietnam); found **lack of assistive technology for children with ADHD in Indonesia**. ASEAN policy brief highlights role of social enterprises for disability inclusion and rights.
- **Example**: IT for Inclusion in Vietnam — social enterprise empowering people with disabilities through IT careers and digital skills.
- **Published revenue data for SE Asia disability edtech**: None found in search.
— [EdTech Hub SE Asia disability review](https://docs.edtechhub.org/lib/M6A4ZHJ3); [ASEAN social enterprises and disability](https://eria.org/research/social-enterprises-and-disability-promoting-inclusion-innovation-and-entrepreneurship-in-asean/)

### Inferences

- **Government contracts and quotas are most reliable revenue source for disability edtech at scale**: US Dept of Education contract ($42.5M Benetech) and federal quota (APH $22.6M annually) provide predictable, large-scale funding without monetizing users. Model is strongest for established nonprofits with compliance/audit infrastructure.
- **B2B enterprise/institutional partnerships subsidize free user tier**: Be My Eyes revenue (corporate volunteering, specialized help) allows free unlimited user access; Microsoft's $25M program funds free Seeing AI by leveraging corporate innovation budget, not user data or advertising.
- **Assistive tech is nonprofit-dominated because user base has low ability to pay**: None of the leading blind/low-vision EdTech platforms charge end users. Revenue sources are public funding, corporate CSR, or institutional contracts. Suggests charitable/social enterprise structure is expected for SPED-focused products.
- **Southeast Asia disability edtech market is underfunded and understudied**: Research exists (EdTech Hub, ASEAN policy interest) but no published business models or revenue figures for disability EdTech in SE Asia. Gap represents either opportunity or market failure.

### Gaps

- **No published revenue or pricing data for blind/low-vision assistive tech products in the Philippines or SE Asia**: Unclear if Be My Eyes, Bookshare, or similar operate locally or if there are local alternatives with published pricing.
- **Envisión (subscription assistive tech) pricing and market in SE Asia**: Search did not return Envisión details; unclear if available in Philippines or what subscription cost is.
- **Desmos accessibility funding and revenue model**: Mentioned as example but no funding details found; unclear if Desmos accessibility is grant-funded or tied to Desmos' overall edtech revenue.
- **Sustainability timeline for nonprofit assistive tech without government contracts**: NVDA, Be My Eyes, and others operate with donations/grants; unclear minimum donation level needed to sustain engineering team or if there is a critical size at which volunteer/grant funding becomes insufficient.

## Offline / On-Device AI Economics and Product Funding

### Takeaway

Offline learning platforms serving low-income markets use a social-impact funding mix: large foundation grants (Kolibri: $5M Google.org over 3 years), crowdfunding (Kolibri: $250K Generosity.com, 203% of goal), and nonprofit model where product sales profit funds operations in developing countries (onebillion). Zero marginal inference cost does not directly reduce pricing (both remain free or heavily subsidized) but enables unit economics for low-income deployment (solar tablets, SD cards, LAN servers). 

### Cited Findings

**Kolibri (Learning Equality, UC San Diego Qualcomm Institute, nonprofit)**
- **Technology**: Free offline app providing primary school education content without internet; aligned to local curricula.
- **Funding sources**:
  - **Major grant**: $5 million from Google.org under Global Education Commitment over 3 years (includes in-kind Google expertise and resources).
  - **Crowdfunding**: Generosity.com campaign, $250K goal, raised 203% (donations $1–$426K range).
  - **Total raised since 2013**: $9.5 million in grants, donations, contracts combined.
- **Distribution model**: Offline-capable; no per-user cloud inference cost.
- **Scale target**: Primary school learners in internet-constrained regions.
— [Learning Equality Kolibri Crowdfunding](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/kolibri_crowdfunding_campaign_flies_past_goal_reaching_203_percent); [Google.org $5M Kolibri](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/learning_equality_awarded_5m_through_google.org_global_education_commitment)

**OneBillion (Not-for-profit, onecourse + onetab)**
- **Technology**: Award-winning adaptive software delivering foundational literacy and numeracy via play-based, child-centered pedagogy. Delivered on onetab (low-cost, offline, solar-capable device locked to onecourse). Works without internet.
- **Funding model**: Nonprofit; app sales profits redirected to reach children in developing countries where lack of resources limits education access.
- **Revenue sources**: UK Aid and Comic Relief funding for Malawi deployment (2024 onebillion scaling in Malawi with UK aid).
- **Impact scale**: 2 million+ children learning with onecourse across 6 languages, 17 countries; 9 randomized controlled trials show significant literacy and numeracy gains (girls equal progress to boys).
— [Onebillion about](https://onebillion.org/); [EdTech Hub Malawi scaling](https://edtechhub.org/?p=15024)

**Learning Economics**
- **Zero marginal inference cost implication**: Offline models (Kolibri, onecourse/onetab) eliminate per-user cloud compute cost; enables unit economics for large-scale, low-income deployment. Neither product reduces pricing as a result; remain free or heavily subsidized. Cost savings improve nonprofit sustainability (less donor funding needed per child served).
- **Device strategy**: OneBillion's onetab (solar-powered, locked device) avoids smartphone ownership barrier and supports pay-once-per-device model for schools/NGOs.

### Inferences

- **Foundation grants and crowdfunding, not B2C revenue, fund offline EdTech for low-income learners**: Both Kolibri ($5M Google.org) and onebillion (UK Aid, Comic Relief) rely on institutional/philanthropic funding, not user payment. Suggests offline-first products targeting low-income markets are structurally unsuited to direct monetization.
- **Zero marginal inference cost is operationally invisible to pricing**: Kolibri and onebillion remain free despite no per-user cloud cost. Savings accrue to provider (lower operational cost per child) and funder (more children served per dollar), not end user (no price reduction from what would be free anyway).
- **Device strategy matters for offline reach**: OneBillion's onetab strategy (solar-powered, locked device) sidesteps smartphone cost and OS fragmentation; may explain higher penetration vs. app-only models in very low-resource settings.
- **Hybrid funding (grants + earned revenue) is not yet proven for offline EdTech**: Kolibri and onebillion have not published earned revenue figures; revenue model appears entirely philanthropic. Unclear if any offline EdTech platform has achieved grant-independent sustainability through B2C or B2B sales.

### Gaps

- **Pricing for offline EdTech when monetized (Rumie, eGranary, etc.)**: Search did not return specific pricing or revenue models for other offline learning platforms; unclear if any charge schools/NGOs or end users.
- **Unit economics of offline-first products vs. online equivalents**: No comparative analysis found on cost per child served for offline (Kolibri, onebillion) vs. online (Khan Academy, Quipper) models; unclear if offline cost advantage justifies separate product or if it's a legacy of distribution constraints.
- **Sustainability and donor dependency risk for Kolibri and onebillion**: Both funded by large grants (Google.org, UK Aid, Comic Relief); no published data on transition to earned revenue or donor diversification; unclear if $5M grant is recurring or one-time.

## Sponsor-a-Learner and CSR-Funded Education Models

### Takeaway

CSR-sponsored learning models use a 1:1 cost-transfer structure where paying students, employees, or corporate sponsors fund equivalent access for underserved learners. Monthly sponsorship ranges from €150 (one literacy hub class) to €1,500 (full school). Integration into employee volunteering and CSR reporting is common; measurable impact (students served, completion rates) is a selling point. Model is proven for nonprofit context but requires consistent institutional sponsor pipeline.

### Cited Findings

- **Bayswater's One-for-One Model**: For every week a paying student studies, one week of learning given to disadvantaged student. Aims to give young and disadvantaged people access to essential skills.
— [Bayswater One-for-One Giving](https://bayswater.ac/giving)

- **In Place of War (Creative and Social Entrepreneurship Programme)**: 1:1 business model where student tuition costs fund equivalent education for young people affected by conflict in the Global South.
— [In Place of War CASE programme](https://aspect.ac.uk/?p=7279)

- **Mellon Literacy Corporate Programme**: 1 staff = 1 child sponsorship model; €150/month sponsors one literacy hub class; €1,500/month sponsors full school.
— [Mellon Educate Recurring Donation](https://donate.melloneducate.com/product/recurring-donation-corporate/)

- **Wishes and Blessings Sponsor a Childhood (India, CSR)**: Individuals, organizations, or corporations sponsor cost of one or more aspects of a child's education for inclusive social growth.
— [Sponsor a Childhood Delhi CSR](https://www.csrbox.org/India_organization_project_Delhi-Sponsor-a-Childhood_8345)

- **Gnowbe CSR Integration**: Learning platform allows clients to donate percentage of learning investment to fund content distribution to underserved populations; measurable CSR impact integrated into platform.
— [Gnowbe Gnow Me Gnow You](https://gnowbe.com/gnow-me-gnow-you)

### Inferences

- **1:1 model is psychologically aligned with corporate CSR budgets**: Sponsor-a-learner messaging is clearer than abstract grant funding; appeals to employee volunteering (Mellon, In Place of War) and corporate social responsibility reporting (Gnowbe measurable impact).
- **Pricing scales with institutional buyer capacity**: Bayswater (B2C education) vs. Mellon (B2B corporate literacy) have different per-learner costs (exact B2C price not published, but B2B€150–€1,500/month suggests B2B margin). Suggests can be integrated into B2C and B2B models.
- **CSR integration requires measurement and reporting infrastructure**: Gnowbe's platform-integrated donation and Mellon's tracking suggest that sponsor-a-learner models require transparent outcome tracking (students served, completion, skills gained) to satisfy corporate ESG/CSR compliance.
- **Model is complementary to subscription or institutional revenue, not replacement**: No examples found of 1:1 sponsorship as sole revenue source; typically paired with direct student fees (Bayswater, In Place of War) or corporate learning subscriptions (Gnowbe).

### Gaps

- **Philippine-based sponsor-a-learner or CSR education programs**: Search did not return examples of working 1:1 or CSR-sponsored models in the Philippines; unclear if corporate sponsorship culture or ESG reporting appetite in PH market is sufficient to sustain model.
- **Donor retention and churn in sponsor-a-learner models**: No data found on monthly sponsorship retention rates or what causes sponsor dropout; unclear if model is stable long-term or prone to boom-bust cycles.
- **Pricing elasticity for CSR sponsorship in low-income markets**: Mellon model (€150–€1,500/month) is EU-market pricing; unclear if similar per-learner cost would work for CSR sponsors in SE Asia or if price must be substantially lower.

## Key Takeaways for Pakinig Business Model

### Synthesis Across Models

1. **Government procurement (DepEd) is viable but requires scale and repeat-vendor trust**: Khan Academy reached 711K learners via DepEd + telco partnership (free user tier funded by Smart data subsidy). Quipper operates B2C and DepEd models in parallel. Risk: vendor consolidation favors incumbents; Pakinig's advantage is SPED-first differentiation (accessibility by design, offline-capable for rural areas).

2. **Free or freemium user tier is market expectation in Philippine EdTech**: Khan Academy free, Quipper free basic tier, Google Workspace 1M licenses at-cost via DepEd procurement. B2C monetization (Quipper 300 PHP/month) serves middle-class aspirational segment; SPED market may require higher subsidy ratio.

3. **Assistive tech for blind/low-vision learners is nonprofit/CSR-funded, not B2C**: Be My Eyes, Bookshare, NVDA, Seeing AI all free to users; revenue from B2B enterprise (Be My Eyes for Work), government contracts (Bookshare $42.5M US Dept Education), or corporate innovation budgets (Microsoft $25M). User-pays model does not exist for this market. Implication: expect Pakinig to operate as nonprofit or CSR-funded entity if targeting blind learners; price discovery unlikely.

4. **Offline-first EdTech for low-income markets requires philanthropic funding**: Kolibri ($5M Google.org + $250K crowdfunding), onebillion (UK Aid, Comic Relief). Zero marginal inference cost does not translate to lower pricing or B2C viability; instead lowers provider operating cost. Suggests Pakinig's offline advantage (Whisper on-device, no cloud) is operational not commercial; reduces cost-of-serving but does not unlock B2C market.

5. **Telco data partnerships are leverage point for scale in Philippines**: Khan Academy's Smart subsidy (free data) de-risked adoption; could apply to Pakinig if partnered with telco (Smart, Globe, DITO) for DepEd bulk deployment.

6. **CSR and sponsor-a-learner models are complementary, not primary**: 1:1 sponsorship (€150–€1,500/month institutional sponsors) works for B2B educational services (Mellon, Bayswater, In Place of War) and corporate CSR (Gnowbe). Applicable to SPED programs if corporate ESG sponsors can be identified; not a primary revenue driver.

### Recommended Model Mix for Pakinig (based on research)

- **Primary: Government / institutional funding** (DepEd procurement, DSWD disability programs, foundation grants like Ayala, GiveWell-aligned organizations)
- **Secondary: Telco partnership for data subsidy** (Smart/Globe data subsidy to learners, similar to Khan Academy model)
- **Tertiary: CSR and sponsor-a-learner** (corporate ESG funding for blind-learner cohorts; Mellon-style €150+/month per learner for SPED schools)
- **User tier: Free** (per market expectation and assistive tech precedent; no B2C subscription pricing for blind learners identified in research)

**Offline-first advantage**: Reduces cloud inference cost and enables rural deployment without dependency on stable internet; operationally valuable but not a consumer pricing differentiator.
